### An Overview and Tips for Novices (pages 12 and 13)

The overview defines the form in the house's flat, encouraging voice — interactive fiction "is a story in which
*you* are the main character", the player moves by typing directions and reads the descriptions carefully, and
puzzles are ordinary obstacles: *"You should think of a locked door or a ferocious beast not as a permanent
obstacle, but merely as a puzzle to be tackled."*

**Tips for Novices**, the nine pointers, is the page a companion and a player should both have read:

> 1. Draw a map. It should include each location, the directions connecting it to other locations, and any
> interesting objects there. [...] Note that there are 10 possible directions, plus IN and OUT.
> 2. Read the story carefully. There are often clues in the descriptions of locations and objects, as well as
> in labels, engravings, books, and so on. Even strange or dangerous actions may provide clues, and might
> prove to be fun!

The page also says to save your place often, that most objects you can pick up matter to one or more puzzles,
and that any command can be worded many ways — PUT ON THE GOWN, WEAR THE TATTY DRESSING GOWN or DON GOWN, since
WEAR alone is enough when only one garment is present.

### Communicating with HITCHHIKER'S (pages 14 and 15)

The parser rules, which are worth having exactly:

> When you finish typing a sentence, press the RETURN (or ENTER) key. HITCHHIKER'S will respond by telling you
> whether your request is possible at this point in the story and what happened as a result.
>
> HITCHHIKER'S recognizes your words by their first six letters, and all subsequent letters are ignored.
> Therefore, BULLDOg, BULLDOgs, BULLDOzer, and BULLDOckpokingham (a small town in Dockpokinghamshire) would all
> be treated as the same word by HITCHHIKER'S.

Movement is by the eight compass directions plus UP/DOWN and IN/OUT; *"On board a ship, you'll want to use the
directions PORT (or P), STARBOARD (or SB), FORE (or F), and AFT."* Sentences may be joined with THEN or a full
stop, objects with AND or a comma, and ALL means "every visible object except those inside something else". The
three kinds of question answered are **WHAT**, **WHERE** and **WHO**, and commands may be given to characters by
naming them first. Unrecognised words are scenery: HITCHHIKER'S "recognizes over 800 words".

## The Order for Destruction, the copy in the other script

The second copy is the same form — same layout, same tick boxes, same illustration, same seal — but every word
of it is printed in an invented alphabet: a runic or alien lettering of angular and curved characters, several
of them resembling Cyrillic or Greek, laid out in exactly the positions the English copy gives its text. The
compiler of these notes can find no English word on it. What is legible is the structure: the huge outline
title; the four-line preamble with blanks where the English copy gives the day, the month, the year, the house,
the town and the number of days; a first list of four tick boxes, none ticked; a second list of eight, with the
single tick already made in pen, in the same position as the *"It's in the way."* tick on the yellow copy; an
illustration that is *not* of Arthur Dent's house — a large planet in contour-line relief, hanging in a hatched
black sky; and the same seal with a three-line authorisation block over signatures drawn as unreadable marks.

This is one of the **"DESTRUCT ORDERS FOR YOUR HOME AND PLANET"** the brochure advertises — "Suitable for
framing, and great gag gifts at any party!"
