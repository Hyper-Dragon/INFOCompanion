*What came in the Enchanter box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The scans in this set are the box face, a photograph of the box's contents, the **Enchanters' Guild
pin**, a parchment scroll scanned twice, and the manual itself only in part: the cover of
**A Brief History of Magic** by **Gustar Woomax**, its pages 2 through 8, and one further page
printed in pink and blue with no page number on it. The manual's own table of contents (page 11),
its command list and its spell pages are **not** among the scans, so nothing here is quoted from
them.

Nor do the scans carry any platform, disk-format, publisher, ISBN or copyright line. The only
printed packaging line on the box face is **First in the ENCHANTER series**; the only copyright
notice in the whole set is the booklet cover's in-fiction one. A player reading this file should
know that the edition itself is not identifiable from these scans.

## The box face

The artwork is a single creature on a flat khaki-grey ground: an angular, spined thing built out of
blue and violet bands — chevrons, step patterns, interlace knotwork in purple and black — with a
pale yellow crescent moon set into one blue panel, a spoked wheel-and-circle device at its left
end, and a scattering of small geometric ornaments. It reads as equal parts dragon, arrowhead and
illuminated initial.

Below the creature, in small capitals:

> When a dark and powerful evil usurps the land. Grasp this reliquary. Sealed within, find the
> wisdom that can guide you. The brave novice who shall be hailed

The scan crops the copy tight; the sentence ends at *hailed* and no further line follows it before
the title, so the blurb as photographed stops there.

Under that, the word **ENCHANTER** is drawn in outline capitals, each letter built like a piece of
clockwork — circles crossed by straight lines, an axle and spokes inside the O, a small starburst
inside the H — followed by the trademark mark. At the foot of the face, in small type:

> First in the ENCHANTER series

## The photograph of the contents

The box back is given over to a photograph of the package's contents on a wooden surface in warm
lamplight, arranged like a still life of a sorcerer's desk:

* a **scroll**, rolled and tied, lying at the top left;
* a sheet of **parchment, sealed with a red wax seal**, on which the invocation quoted below is
  visible in the same curly lettering as the scroll scan;
* a **black quill** and a **faceted glass ink bottle** with a black stopper, in the lower right;
* the **pink-covered booklet**, its cover design — the large gilt-outlined sigil in a decorated
  panel — showing beneath the title **A Brief History of Magic**;
* a dark square **jacket or sleeve** bearing a pale label printed **ENCHANTER**, with the dark disc
  beneath it: the game in its packaging;
* and, along the lower left of the photograph, a block of printed copy.

That last block is **illegible**: at the scan's resolution the lines resolve into dark smudges with
no readable letterforms at all. Whether it is the box's contents list or a caption is not
recoverable from this scan.

## The Enchanters' Guild pin

An oval pin — the folder holds the same photograph twice, as a `.jpg` and a smaller `.gif` — about
the size of a thumbnail, and the only metal object in the package. Its field is dark, close to black
with a green cast, laid into a gilt rim. On it, in gilt relief:

* the **Guild sigil**: a tall, squared letter like an open **C** or **G**, cross-barred, with a
  small dragon's head at the end of the bar and a cage of curved gilt bands over a circle behind it
  — the same device that appears on the booklet cover and inside the letters of the box title;
* a small **crescent moon** to the right of the sigil;
* an arc of tiny repeated ornament inside the curve of the sigil that may be lettering, but at this
  resolution it reads only as a dotted chain and **nothing on it can be made out**;
* a **round gilt stud or rivet** at the left, the pin's fixing.

It is a lapel badge rather than a prop with a use: the mark of the Circle a novice is working
towards, in the same form as the emblem on the manual.

## The scroll of the Circle of Enchanters

One parchment, scanned twice. The scan of the front is a torn, landscape fragment — the sheet is
thin enough that the print of the reverse side shows through it as faint mirror-image lines — and
carries a single invocation set in large curly lettering, opening with a paragraph mark and a
decorated initial:

> If you truly are the one brave enough to face unspeakable peril, let your wizardly powers reveal
> our words.

The second scan is the body of the message, a long narrow sheet with an illuminated initial at the
head, printed in the same archaic face. It runs, in full:

> We the Circle of Enchanters, have foreseen that a ruthless and powerful evil may one day seize
> this land. Should that time arrive, we also foresee the coming of age of a young Enchanter, one
> whose heart and wits may triumph over the Warlock's dark necromancy. It is our hope that this
> Enchanter hears our words.
>
> We cannot see your face through the mists of time, but this we know in truth… You are promising
> in magic but have not gained your full skills. That is as it must be, for the Warlock would
> recognize one of the Council of Elders and would sense the presence of a more powerful Enchanter.
> Thus, in your obscurity you may find your way to his lair before he knows the mortal danger you
> pose.
>
> Four spells will arm you as you begin your quest. FROTZ shall turn darkness to light. NITFOL shall
> build a bridge of language to all the animals of the land. GUSTO writes magic; by it you shall
> commit spells to your treasured spell book. BLORB is a spell of protection; with it you may guard
> your most precious possession.
>
> Never forget, young Enchanter, that magic is your only weapon. By your vows to the Council of
> Elders you have cast aside the common protections of sword and armor. Therefore you will need many
> more spells to accomplish your quest. These will be revealed to you. Always be alert.
>
> Though your way will be harsh and your steps fraught with danger, remember throughout your quest
> that you are the only hope of this land. We pray that our successors will choose wisely and that
> you will prove yourself worthy of the title Enchanter. Signed with our mark and cast into the
> ages…
>
> The Circle of Enchanters

## A Brief History of Magic

### The cover

Pink board, with a decorated panel in blue and violet: an interlace border around a squared
sigil-letter rendered in outline with a crescent moon and a run of small round stones set into it,
under which appear the title, the author, and this notice:

> A BRIEF HISTORY OF MAGIC
>
> By Gustar Woomax
>
> Copied Right in 927 G.U.E. All rights reversed.
> A POPULAR ENCHANTING Book.

The same sigil is what the pin carries and what the outline capitals of the box title are built
from, so a player who has opened the box has already met it three times.

### Page 2 — the four ages

The page is a drawn scroll-sheet with an illuminated square initial. It sets the booklet's plan:

> The history of magic can be broken down into four distinct periods: the Empirical Age, the
> Scientific Age, the Industrial Age, and the Age of Guilds. Each of these will be explored in some
> depth in order to give the reader a sense of the course of events which has shaped the use of
> magic in today's society.

The pages are numbered at the foot; the eight pages scanned run 2 to 8 without a gap.

### The Empirical Age (page 3)

Set on a starry field with a smoking brazier at the head and chains of ornamented circles along the
foot, and the chapter title cut into a band of dotted cross-hatching:

> Primitive cultures had naturally assumed that the disorderly nature of our world was due to such
> supernatural causes as magic. With the founding of the "natural" sciences, however, nature was
> increasingly viewed as being orderly. As the sciences progressed, the knowledge and lore of magic
> largely disappeared. As early as the 5th century GUE (Great Underground Empire), however, such
> students of the mystic arts as Bizboz and Dinbar thoroughly examined ancient writings on the
> subject. Bizboz himself wrote what became the seminal work in Thaumaturgy, "On the Presence of
> Incredibly Weird Stuff Going On," in 473 GUE, in which he claimed to have discovered
> "for-the-most-part Natural Rules" by which this "Weird Stuff" is ordered.
>
> This work was ridiculed by the leading scholars of the time, leading to Bizboz's removal from the
> faculty at the Galepath University, and, eventually, to his tragic suicide in 475 GUE. His work,
> however, encouraged others in the pursuit of magical knowledge, with mixed results. Charlatans,
> claiming to have created magical potions and powders, regularly fooled the gullible population
> into buying potions which claimed to do such things as "reverse hair loss" and "draw Trebled
> Fromps in Double Fanucci." Such appeals to public ignorance led King Duncanthrax in 672 GUE to
> write the Unnatural Acts, which provided stiff penalties for those convicted of selling "Unnatural
> or Supernatural substances."

### The Scientific Age (page 4)

The chapter title hangs from a drawn branch with an owl and a bat at the corners of the page:

> While the charlatans were at work, serious students took up the cause of magic, attempting to
> explain the natural world as a by-product of the interrelated workings of the sciences of
> Physics, Medicine, Chemistry, Mathematics, and Thaumaturgy. Their success in demonstrating the
> so-called first principles of Thaumaturgy, namely Presence, Incantation, and Unusual Effect, led
> to a loosening of the Unnatural Acts to allow what became known as Scientific Thaumaturgy. During
> this period, the first chapter of the Guild of Enchanters was founded at the tiny hamlet of
> Accardi-by-the-Sea by the great thaumaturge, Vilboz.
>
> During the reign of Frobwit the Flatter (701–727 GUE), the art and science of Thaumaturgy
> flourished. The first reliable Incantation Device, known to scholars as the Hyperbolic Incantation
> Concentrator, was produced at the Thaumaturgical Institute in 723 GUE. The long, thin, portable
> device, nicknamed the "magic wand" by the lay press, became an instant sensation among the
> populace, and gained a certain measure of respect for the fledgling science.
>
> A major advance in Thaumaturgy occurred when Davmar, working in newly-crowned King Mumberthrax
> Flathead's laboratory, discovered a means by which Incantation could be stored on special
> Presence-imbued paper. These so-called scrolls were found, however, to be destroyed during the
> spells' Incantation. Nonetheless, scrolls soon replaced the temperamental and poorly-understood
> "wand" as the primary means of Incantation.
>
> The problem of imbuing Presence became a deterrent to the rapid growth of magical science. The
> creation of a single powerful scroll could take literally months for even the most creative and
> productive thaumaturge. This roadblock prevented the widespread use of magic for generations.

### The Industrial Age (page 5)

The page is drawn as an unrolled scroll, and is the page that explains where spell books and potions
come from:

> The Industrial Age dawned in 769 GUE with a discovery by a little-known thaumaturge named Berzio.
> Berzio, working for years in his own self-made workshop and often going for days without food,
> drink, or sleep, created the means by which Presence could be transferred from a scroll to a
> specially impregnated paper by use of a simple spell, which he named after his dog, Gnusto. This
> paper, in turn, held the Presence even after the Incantation had been finished, solving the major
> problem in spell production. The euphoria which greeted this discovery was tempered by the finding
> that very powerful spells could not be transferred in this way. Nevertheless, spell "books," which
> were capable of holding dozens of spells, were produced in great number, leading to the founding
> of a new industry.
>
> Another advance in Thaumaturgy occurred with the finding that certain liquids and powders could be
> imbued with the magical Presence. Such potions are of great interest, although their limitations
> have prevented them from supplanting scrolls as the primary method of Incantation. The first of
> these potions, which obviates the need for food and drink, was given the name BERZIO, in honor of
> the great thaumaturge.

### The Age of Guilds (page 6)

A page whose central illustration — a vine with acorns and a growing tree — overlays the text, so a
few words sit on top of the line art. The passage is the one that explains why magic is licensed:

> As the use of magic became more prevalent, so did the problems inherent in its use. Since magic
> had become available to people in all professions, conflicts arose. One famous issue involved the
> question of whether the plumber's FIZMO spell ("cause stopped-up pipes to unclog") could be sold
> as a digestive aid by physicians. The issue came to a head in the aftermath of the Endless Fire
> of 773 GUE, so named because it burned for 4 weeks after destroying the city of Mareilon. It was
> later found to have been started by a civil servant who thought he was casting the ZEMDOR spell
> ("turn original into triplicate") but who, instead, cast the ZIMBOR spell ("turn one really big
> city into lots of tiny, little ashes").
>
> This led Lord Dirwitt Flathead (the Excessive) to issue a series of 5,521 edicts over the
> following few weeks, which had the effect of severely limiting access to magic (and, incidentally,
> lawyers). Henceforth, all magic was entrusted to the various Guilds of Enchanters, which by now
> existed in many small communities. Each Guild, whose elders comprised the so-called Circle of
> Enchanters, was empowered to form schools for the training of new Enchanters. This official
> sanctioning of the Guilds led to the formation of numerous other chapters, with membership in the
> various Guilds in excess of 2,000 by the year 800 GUE. Despite the fall of the Great Underground
> Empire in 883 GUE under the feeble-minded reign of Wurb Flathead, the Guild of Enchanters remains
> virtually unchanged in character today.

### Today's Enchanter (page 7)

The page is scattered with small stars and a bird's foot, with an eagle perched on a chalice at its
foot. This is the page that states the rules of magic a player is working under:

> Since the fall of the Empire, magic has again become a mysterious art, practiced primarily by
> trained Sorcerers, although a few spells, such as UMBOZ ("obviate need for dusting") and NERZO
> ("balance checkbook"), have been approved for over-the-counter sale. Upon graduation from an
> accredited Thaumaturgical College, an Enchanter is given a spell book with a few spells, none of
> which has great power. As an Enchanter continues his or her studies, new spells may be obtained;
> these may be copied into a spell book for use whenever the occasion warrants.
>
> The Enchanter's job is not as easy as is commonly thought. An Enchanter must memorize a spell
> written in a spell book before casting it. (Spells on scrolls and those which have been
> permanently etched in the memory by training needn't be memorized.) Moreover, if an Enchanter
> needs to use a particular spell twice, it must be memorized twice, since the effort of casting it
> makes it a jumble in one's memory. In fact, even a night's sleep will make an Enchanter forget any
> memorized spells. But, in spite of the rigors of spell casting, the personal rewards are great,
> and the job of Enchanter remains a popular and well-respected vocation.

### An Afterthought (page 8)

Decorated with what looks like a draftsman's diagram of the Guild sigil — the creature drawn as a
nautilus-and-cone with lettered points **a** to **g** and an apple at the corner — and carrying the
booklet's closing quotation:

> The most fitting words regarding the history of magic were written over a century ago by the
> renowned historian Ozmar in 821 GUE. He wrote: "The greatest irony is this: that the ancients of
> our kind were nearer to knowing the truth about Science than those who called themselves
> Scientists. Science has taught us much and given us new words for old mysteries. But beneath these
> words are mysteries, and beneath them more mysteries. The pursuit of Magic has given these
> mysteries meaning and provided for our people great benefits unrealized as yet by Science. One
> day, perhaps, a great union will be formed between Magic and Science, and the final mysteries will
> be solved."

### The prophecy page

One further page is scanned, printed in the same pink and blue as the cover rather than on the white
text pages, and it carries **no page number**. It is the piece of printed matter addressed directly
to the player:

> Our once peaceful land is being held in thrall by a warlock so powerful, he detects and destroys
> even the strongest opponents who enter his domain. But all is not yet lost, for an ancient
> prophecy speaks of a brave and cunning novice enchanter, someone insignificant enough to slip past
> the watchful warlock and conquer him by means of rudimentary magic and spells discovered during
> the quest.
>
> Our only hope for the future is you. Should you succeed, a seat in the illustrious Circle of
> Enchanters shall be yours. But if you fail, our land will be subjugated to unimaginable doom
> forever. Farewell, brave enchanter; may your necromancy save the land and the prophecy prove true.
>
> The Table of Contents for the instruction manual is on page 11. Take a look at it to find out what
> you need to know before you start the story.

That last sentence is the only pointer in the whole package to where the manual's instructions
begin, and the pages it points at are not in this scan set.

## The spells the printed pages name

Every spell word the package prints, with what the package says it does — the four the scroll
promises a novice, and the ones the history mentions in passing. Nothing here is advice about how
to use them.

* **FROTZ** — "shall turn darkness to light" (scroll).
* **NITFOL** — "shall build a bridge of language to all the animals of the land" (scroll).
* **GUSTO** — "writes magic; by it you shall commit spells to your treasured spell book" (scroll).
* **BLORB** — "is a spell of protection; with it you may guard your most precious possession"
  (scroll).
* **Gnusto** — the simple spell Berzio "named after his dog", by which Presence is transferred from
  a scroll onto impregnated paper (page 5).
* **FIZMO** — the plumber's spell, "cause stopped-up pipes to unclog" (page 6).
* **ZEMDOR** — "turn original into triplicate" (page 6).
* **ZIMBOR** — "turn one really big city into lots of tiny, little ashes" (page 6).
* **UMBOZ** — "obviate need for dusting" (page 7).
* **NERZO** — "balance checkbook" (page 7).
* **BERZIO** — not a spell but a potion, "which obviates the need for food and drink", named in
  honour of the thaumaturge (page 5).

## What the player had on the table

Four pieces of printed matter, and each does a different job. The box face gives the situation in
one sentence. The scroll gives the four spells a novice starts with, by name, with a one-line
statement of what each is for, and the promise that the rest will be revealed later. The manual's
history pages explain the machinery of magic a player has to live with — Presence, Incantation and
Unusual Effect; spells memorised before casting; two castings requiring two memorisations; a
night's sleep erasing the lot; scrolls and permanently etched spells being the exceptions — and the
prophecy page states the stake plainly. The pin carries the Guild's mark and nothing else.

There is no walkthrough in any of it, and no puzzle answer: the printed material sets out the rules
of the world and the spells that exist in it, and leaves the rest to the person at the keyboard.
