*What came in the Hollywood Hijinx box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box lists its contents in the caption to a photograph on the back — *"Featured in every package
of HOLLYWOOD HIJINX: your HOLLYWOOD HIJINX disk; a star-studded copy of **TINSELWORLD** magazine;
your Aunt Hildegarde's will; an autographed photo of your Uncle Buddy; and a lucky palm tree swizzle
stick."* The photograph shows them all lying on a Hollywood sidewalk beside Buddy Burbank's star:
the disk in its sleeve, the magazine, the photograph, the folded will, a cocktail glass and the green
swizzle stick.

## The box, in its own words

**The front** is a yellow-and-orange collage of Hollywood: **HOLLYWOOD** in great gold-edged letters
that double as a film strip — faces in every letter, a woman screaming in one, a couple kissing in
another — over a postcard of a dark hillside house with two round green porch lights like eyes, palm
trees and a desert sunset behind it. **HIJINX** follows in green; the Infocom logo rises in a
technicolor sunburst at the top left with a small plane buzzing past it, and the word **PRESENTS**
sits beneath it.

**The back** is the pitch — and it is where the terms of the game are stated, so it matters:

> *Vampire Penguins. A Corpse Line. Meltdown on Elm Street.* Who could forget these classic
> Hollywood movies produced by your uncle, Buddy Burbank? But his greatest masterpiece has yet to be
> experienced … HOLLYWOOD HIJINX, starring **you**!
>
> Your Uncle Buddy and Aunt Hildegarde have passed away, but their memory lives on in their Malibu
> mansion, filled with a lifetime of Hollywood memorabilia. And you've inherited it all, with one
> stipulation — you can only claim your booty if you find the treasures hidden throughout the
> sprawling beachfront estate. **If you can't find the treasures in one night, you lose the whole
> caboodle.**
>
> It's just the sort of thing you'd expect from Aunt Hildegarde and Uncle Buddy. And their home is
> familiar territory: you spent your childhood summers there with your Cousin Herman. Although some
> say the house is haunted, you're not fooled. You know that Uncle Buddy, who wore a different
> polyester leisure suit each day of the week, was always rigging the place with goofy gags and
> booby traps.
>
> Inside the house, everything is just as glitzy and full of fun as you remember it to be. There's
> the luxurious private screening room, the gold-plated bathroom faucets in the shape of Oscars, and
> the wacky props from old Buddy Burbank movies.
>
> The Malibu estate seems like a funhouse at first. But the puzzles you must solve prove that Aunt
> Hildegarde and Uncle Buddy weren't just kidding around. **Claiming the Burbank bundle turns out to
> be quite a challenge — and it's all in your honor.**
>
> HOLLYWOOD HIJINX is as zany as its author, **"Hollywood" Dave Anderson**. It's cram-packed with
> puzzles that will test the wits and tickle the ribs of both first time and experienced players.

Publisher's block: Infocom, 125 CambridgePark Drive, Cambridge, MA 02140; © 1986 Infocom, Inc.;
ISBN 0-87321-363-7; part numbers **G-IA4-02**, **IA4-AP1**, **TD-IA4**; *HOLLYWOOD HIJINX is a
trademark of Infocom, Inc.*

## Aunt Hildegarde's will

A single sheet on her own letterhead — **Hildegarde Montague Burbank**, *Hildebud*, Malibu,
California 90265 — written out by hand in a large, sweeping script. It is the game's rulebook, and
nothing in the box states the terms anywhere else:

> Well, Pumpkin, I've finally gone to join Buddy in Paradise. I sure had a lot of fun in life, but
> nothing lasts forever, eh? I will miss Hildebud, our home for so many years. We all had plenty of
> wonderful times there, didn't we? Well, now it's all going to be yours. I know you love the old
> place as much as I do. You'll take good care of it.
>
> Your Uncle Buddy and I often talked about this and we agreed that everything should go to you. Of
> all our nieces and nephews, you were our favorite. **Buddy gave me the enclosed photo which he
> wanted passed on to you along with this letter.**
>
> Now, dear, we are fond of you, but Buddy and I worked hard to build our business and keep up our
> estate. We don't intend to just throw it all away. So although we both think you are clever enough
> to manage everything wisely, we need to make sure. That's why we've planned a little test for you.
>
> **Hidden around the house and grounds at Hildebud are ten "treasures" from your Uncle Buddy's
> films.** All you need to do is find the treasures in one night, and everything will be yours. Now
> that doesn't sound too hard, does it?
>
> If you can't find the treasures, too bad, sweetie! We'll give our other nieces and nephews a crack
> at it. **The clever shall inherit the bundle.** So that everyone knows what's up, a letter is being
> sent to each one of you.
>
> My lawyer will pick you up at 8:00 on the evening of the funeral and drive you to Hildebud. Good
> luck, Pumpkin. Make your Aunt Hildegarde proud.
>
> Being of sound mind and body, I do hereby sign this testament in the presence of two witnesses.

Signed **Hildegarde Montague Burbank**, with two witnesses — the second signature is a hurried
scrawl that reads as **Buck Palace**, the third is **Dorothy LaFlank** — all three dated **6/18/86**.

Three facts fall out of it that the rest of the box only implies: the number is **ten**; the things
to look for are **treasures from the films**, not objects in general; and the clock starts at **eight
o'clock on the night of the funeral**, with the night in which to work.

## The autographed photograph of Uncle Buddy

An eight-by-ten studio portrait in black and white, signed across the top right in white ink:
*"Regards, **Buddy Burbank**"*. Buddy is photographed looking up and off to one side, eyebrows
raised, mouth open in a performer's grin, in a wide-collared patterned shirt with a neck chain and a
bracelet.

The photograph is one half of the puzzle's key. **Its back carries a poem in Buddy's hand** — a
farewell note to the same niece or nephew the will addresses, and, read carefully, a set of hints for
three of the ten treasures:

> Hey, kiddo!
>
> The night will open a few doors for ya!
>
> **Be brave as~~Sheriff Roy~~ in "Fastest Blunder in the West,"**
> The day the rustlin' outlaws put their chainsaws to the test.
> Old Sheriff Roy used whip, then chop, then liquify, puree,
> But his blunder was no match for the Chainsaw's mean foray.
> As pieces of Roy scattered, we knew that we had trouble.
> We hadn't switched the sheriff with his plastic life-size double!
>
> **Be bold as Captain Bot in "Cannibal Buffet of the East,"**
> Who agreed to come to dinner, not knowing he was the feast.
> Though how d'oeuvres were greasy, sauteed eyeballs weren't so hot,
> But their mouths began to water when they put Bot in the pot.
> Don't feel bad for Captain Bot whose agent was a real smarty.
> He signed Bot for a sequel called "Cannibal Meatloaf Party."
>
> **Be clever like the tailor in "Vampire Penguins of the North."**
> A cummerbund for each penguin was the plan he had set forth.
> The first penguins that he met were of Transylvanian extraction,
> And their fondness for his neck was a natural reaction.
> Now the tailor's up in heaven, sewing angel gowns of white,
> While the penguins search the frozen turf for other necks to bite.
>
> We sure enjoyed having you with us all those summers.
> You always were my favorite!
>
> Love ya, kiddo!
>
> **Uncle Buddy**

Three titles, three characters, three deaths, and one clue in each stanza — the life-size double, the
sequel, the cummerbund — which is as much as the box says about how the treasures are hidden. Note
that Buddy's handwriting uses strikethroughs and corrections, so a reader who has the original will
see the poem as a working draft rather than a fair copy.

## The palm tree swizzle stick

A green plastic cocktail stirrer, about a hand long, moulded as a palm tree — a fan of fronds on a
curving, bark-textured stem. It came stuck in the box with the photograph and the will, and it exists
in the fiction as well as on the table: the last page of *Tinselworld* is an advertisement for the
very object, and it is the reason the "Hidden Treasure" ad is in the magazine at all (see below).

## TINSELWORLD, Vol. 35, No. 27

The magazine, in full — the *"SPECIAL BUDDY BURBANK MEMORIAL ISSUE"* — and the richest thing in the
box after the will, because a companion who has read it knows the films, the family and the
house. The cover is a photograph of **Hildegarde Burbank** in a spangled black top, holding a
champagne flute, under the banner headline **HILDY TAKES FINAL BOW**, with *"DOC'S AMAZING
DISCOVERY: DILL PICKLES FLUSH OUT CHOLESTEROL, INCREASE STAMINA!"* across the top and three more
cover lines down the left and bottom: *"CRAZED GERBIL ATTACKS GRAMPS"*, *"DOROTHY LAFLANK REVEALS:
I'M MAD ABOUT BUCK!"*, and the masthead — **TINSELWORLD**, *"NEWS FROM HOLLYWOOD AND AROUND THE
GLOBE"*, Vol. 35, No. 27.

### The family history, as the magazine tells it

**"Chapter in Hollywood History Draws to a Close"** (special to *Tinselworld* by Winona Sullivan) is
the obituary that doubles as the backstory:

> With the death of Hildegarde Burbank last Tuesday at her Malibu beachfront bungalow, a chapter in
> Hollywood history drew to a close. Hildegarde was the "Queen" behind mogul Buddy Burbank, the
> "King of the B's."
>
> Burbank, who died several years ago of a massive heart attack, produced, directed, and starred in
> numerous movies released by his **Buddy Burbank Studios**. Although some say the flicks are of no
> real value, others recognize them as the high point of low-budget films.
>
> Buddy and Hildegarde first met in **1948** when Buddy was a young actor with **GUM**. Yearning to
> oversee his own studio, he approached Hildegarde's father, wealthy financier **Curtis Montague**.
> Horrified by the young man's plan to offer post-war America a series of low-grade, light
> entertainment films, the cultivated "Monty" turned him down.
>
> The next day, Hildegarde appeared at Buddy's dressing room on the GUM lot. **Twelve years his
> senior**, she was a former debutante living a humdrum existence of afternoon teas and charity
> benefits. She also yearned for something of her own: a life that would better match her feisty
> nature and taste for adventure.
>
> Hildegarde offered Buddy unlimited use of her ample trust fund to start his own studio, provided
> she had a hand in its operation. There was only one problem — Hildegarde could not draw from her
> fund until she was married. Luckily, the stately Hildegarde answered Buddy's dreams in more ways
> than one. He proposed that very afternoon and they were **quietly married in Las Vegas the
> following week.**
>
> Despite her father's recriminations, Hildegarde went on to prove that she had made a good
> investment. **Buddy Burbank Studios rapidly rose to acclaim with such classic hits as *Attack of
> the Killer Rutabagas*, *Bees Do It*, and *And It Came from the Neighbor's House*.** The studio's
> prominence continued through the 60's, riding the crest of the wave of low-budget films.
>
> Hollywood celebrities flocked to **Hildebud**, the palatial oceanfront bungalow the couple built in
> trendy Malibu. The luxurious home was decorated with memorabilia from Buddy Burbank film hits as
> well as souvenirs from the couple's travels around the globe. Hildegarde's rose garden was long the
> envy of the Malibu community, and their private beachfront was the scene of many a star-studded
> luau.
>
> The couple never had any children, deciding that the studio was enough of a job to nurture. They
> were, however, very close to their numerous nieces and nephews, who were often invited to spend
> holidays with "Uncle Buddy and Aunt Hildegarde."
>
> When the craze for "B" movies died down in the 1970's, Buddy introduced a new star in the form of
> **Buck Palace, the fighting letter carrier**. With his rock-hard physique, weapons-at-the-ready,
> and determination to fight for justice whatever the weather, Buck was the idol of millions of
> youngsters.
>
> Following Buddy's death, Hildegarde continued to control a majority of Buddy Burbank Studios. She
> also maintained her reputation as a hostess, although her parties were on a much smaller scale than
> before. The past few months, she has been seen about town with **record producer Tony Paoli**, and
> it had been hinted that a marriage proposal was in the offing.
>
> It is not known what will happen to Buddy Burbank Studios without the ample funding of Hildegarde
> Burbank. Details of her will have yet to be released, but **family members are expected to
> inherit**.

**Tinseltown Tattle**, with Shayna Waltz, adds the funeral and the guests:

> In death as in life, it was roses for HILDEGARDE BURBANK. The Malibu hostess was well known for her
> dazzling rose garden, whose luxuriant blooms filled the oceanfront bungalow she shared with late
> hubby BUDDY BURBANK. **At her star-studded funeral last Tuesday at Cathedral in the Pines**,
> Hollywood paid tribute to a great lady with thousands of fragrant coral roses. The blooms
> decorated altar and pews, and covered the elegant coral-colored coffin. Although the casket was
> closed according to Hildy's wishes, the dear departed reportedly wore a matching coral satin gown.
>
> Attending Hildy's funeral and the memorial reception at trendy **Bolla-Bolla** in West Hollywood
> were many of the stars made famous by Buddy Burbank Studios. **BUCK PALACE** arrived carrying two
> dozen roses in a combat helmet which he placed at the head of the casket. **HEIDI KORN**, star of
> innumerable B B Studios westerns, looked stunning in the scarlet taffeta dress she wore in
> *Buckeroo Barmaid*. Not to be missed was **DOROTHY LAFLANK**, whose lovely outfit resembled a
> gigantic coral rosebush.
>
> Rumors are flying as to who will inherit Buddy and Hildy's multi-million estate. Shayna places her
> bet on **"LITTLE HERMAN" BEAUMONT**, favored nephew of the couple who starred in the popular *Little
> Herman TV* series in the late 60's.

**"Who will Inherit the Burbank Bundle?"** (by **Gayle von Syska**, *"intimate friend of Hildegarde
Burbank"*) is a two-page column of speculation that ends on the note the game depends on: *"Hildy
always told me she wanted Hildebud to pass on to someone who would love and care for it as much as
she did… I believe the Burbank fortune will be left to one of them."* The spread also carries
photographs from Gayle's personal album — three of the nieces and nephews on an inflatable raft in the
cove in the mid-sixties (the one on the left is **"Little Herman" Beaumont**), and **Hildebud** in the
fifties: a white clapboard colonial with columns, dormers and a long side porch. The caption records
what the game is played inside: *"Hildebud, palatial home of Hildegarde and Buddy Burbank, as it
looked in the fabulous fifties. **Subsequent alterations included the addition of a private
theater.**"*

### The films, as the magazine describes them

This is the reference list a player would keep beside the keyboard, because the treasures are "from
your Uncle Buddy's films". The memorial issue reviews the career in categories:

* **Buck Palace, the Fighting Letter Carrier** — *"Five fantastic films comprised this series about a
  mild-mannered mailman whose dedication to the Post Office and the American way of life inspired us
  all."* Four are described in detail: **Postage Due**, the first in the series, in which Buck takes
  on the New York Mafia's Five Families, *"a war between the Families as payoffs don't arrive and
  drugs are misdelivered"*, the mob countering by ordering Family-operated dry cleaners to *"triple
  the starch for all postal uniforms"*; **Special Delivery**, the prequel set in 1962, in which Buck
  parachutes into Havana with poisoned Cuban stamps to kill Castro, falls for his secretary, and
  *"swims back to the U.S., but not before fighting and destroying over half the Cuban Navy"*;
  **Address Unknown**, the fourth, in which a letter to a POW is returned by Hanoi and Buck goes to
  Cambodia, disguises servicemen *"as parcels, mails them to the Pentagon, and hopes they'll pay the
  postage due"*; and, by implication, a third and a fifth.
* **Burbank's Classic Horror Films** — **Slash 'n' Chop Sock Hop**, in which band member **Queenie
  Bee** goes berserk at a dance, *"slashing dozens of panic-stricken teens with the stiletto
  concealed in her microphone"*, and the survivors are forced into another Limbo contest in the
  gruesome finale; and **Meltdown on Elm Street**, in which a nuclear power plant malfunctions and
  the survivor-victims' dreams of the *"glowboy"* come true, ending in the Elm Street Cinema where the
  film was banned after several moviegoers *"died of fright"*.
* **Buddy Burbank's WIDE-REACHING Talent** — from special effects to social commentary:
  **The Day The Movies Died**, described as *"tongue-in-cheek bloodbath"* training *"his cinematic
  fire on his many critics in the media"*; a picture of the plot follows — a *"mysterious cult"*
  systematically destroying *"nearly every copy of every film ever made"* in a *"vast salt mine vault
  in Utah"*, with the critics **Cisco and Hebert** sent to disarm a bomb planted in one of the
  canisters, and *"an argument breaks out about how best to disarm it"*; and **Crawl-O-Round**, a
  *"brainy creation of Burbank's"* — *"a series of small ion generators placed throughout the theatre"*
  which *"charge the air molecules"* so that *"the hair on movie-goers' arms to stand on end"*.
  Then **The Seven Dwarves Do Dallas**, described as *"Burbank's first and only step into soft-core
  porn"*, which *"in later years… he denounced as exploitative and apologized to the dwarves of
  Dallas."*
* **"Burbank's Last Film: *A Corpse Line*"** — the feature the box's blurb names first. *"Burbank's
  final achievement, A Corpse Line, was never released to the public. Reportedly, Burbank was viewing
  this film when the Man Upstairs said 'cut.' With his last breath, from a plush theatre seat in his
  screening room, Burbank proclaimed A Corpse Line his masterwork. The film, thought to be worth
  millions to his fans today, was never recovered, and no copy is known to exist."* The page's own
  box note adds: *"This is believed to be the only existing still from Burbank's lost masterpiece, A
  Corpse Line. A close look will reveal many of Hollywood's leading men."* — and the still is a line of
  grinning men in hoods and moustaches, holding meat cleavers.

Also in the issue: **TV Tidbits** (*"Three headed tot wins hearts in Hollywood"* — Susan "Tootsie"
Goldman's three-headed son Jeffrey, signed to a three-million-dollar contract with **GUM** studios);
**"GerbIL TERRORIZES GRAMPS"** (Thomas Veldran of Dallas and a psychotic gerbil); **"Remembering
Buddy Burbank … The Curtain Falls on a Golden Era"**, by **Brian Moriarty**, which records that
*"Buddy Burbank created over 600 films in his lifetime; many were landmarks for Burbank as well as the
film industry"* and closes *"We love ya, Buddy Burbank"*; **StarGazing** by *"Our Man in Hollywood,
Carlo Gelato"*, with Dorothy LaFlank's ambitions and Buck Palace's complaint that under contract to
Burbank *"he had to be a fighting letter carrier 24 hours a day"*.

**And the advertising**, which in this package is not separate from the game: *"I am the Lotto Lady"*
(Ramoa, P.O. Box 327, Forest Hills, NY); *"Out of Luck?"* (send $17.00 to **Rev. Olga**, 1498 Avenue B,
NY); *"DON'T Throw away those NAIL Clippings!"* (send them and $12.00 to **Brother Bob**, R.F.D. 1992,
Hopperville, IN); a full-page **FAT-MELT chewing gum** advertisement addressed to Dr. S. E. Meretzky,
P.O. Box 22, Sudcity, MD; **MIRAHAIR**, the hair-restoration cream sold by sole American distributor
**Stan Dornburg** (before-and-after photographs of Carlo Gelato); and, last, filling the final page of
the memorial issue:

> **You can find HIDDEN TREASURE …in your own home!** — *"Wouldn't you like to have your money
> worries solved forever? … Best of all, get this all WITHOUT WORKING A SINGLE DAY? Now you can, with
> the amazing LUCKY PALM TREE SWIZZLE STICK!"*

It is a long advertisement telling the story of a man whose money troubles ended when a palm tree
swizzle stick appeared on his kitchen table and he used it to stir his orange juice — after which a
necklace fell out of a ceiling panel, a treasure chest came up under his spade, and jewels appeared in
a hollow bar in a coat closet. *"When its owner passes away, a treasure often stays just where it was
put, in a nook or cranny of a house or the secret compartment of a piece of furniture that may change
hands many times!"* The order form is headed **Lucky Palm, 7327 Sunset Blvd. West Hollywood, CA**,
and the object it sells — *"shown actual size"*, at $12.95, or two for $21.95 — is the green palm tree
swizzle stick that came in the box.
