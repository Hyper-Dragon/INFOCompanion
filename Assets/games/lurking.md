*What came in the Lurking Horror box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box lists its contents in a photograph on the back — *"Mouldering inside every LURKING HORROR
package: your LURKING HORROR disk, a guide to George Underwood Edwards Institute of Technology,
and your G.U.E. Tech student identification card."* All three are here: the disk in its sleeve,
**G.U.E. at a Glance — A Guide for Freshmen**, and the **student I.D. card**, front and back. The
instruction manual was not among the scans.

## The box, in its own words

**The front** is the title in torn red letters on a background of red brick and cracked grey
plaster, wrapped around a single enormous eye; in the pupil of the eye stands a gaunt figure in
dark robes and a top hat, with a face like a skull. Two roundels: *"BY DAVE LEBLING — CO-AUTHOR OF
ZORK"*, and the Infocom mark. Along the bottom: *"INFOCOM INTERACTIVE FICTION"*, *"Distributed by
Activision (UK) Ltd"*, *"SOFTWARE FOR YOUR AMIGA, 3.5" DISK"*, and a promotion sticker —
Lebling's *Suspect* for $14.95, a $5.00-off offer enclosed.

**The back** is a photograph of a desk: a Macintosh keyboard with the **G.U.E. at a Glance**
booklet propped on the console, the game disk in a black sleeve, an open wallet with the student
I.D. visible inside it, a pen and a notebook, a mouse, a coffee cup, an ashtray, a telephone. The
text beside it sets the whole game up, and it says plainly what the object in the photograph is
for:

> Ever since you arrived at G.U.E. Tech, you've heard stories about the creepy old campus
> basements and storage rooms, some so ancient that they contain only rotting piles of
> unidentifiable junk. Until now, you've never ventured lower than the ground floors of the
> monolithic classroom and dorm buildings, avoiding the warren of tunnels that connect them.
>
> But tonight, something draws you down into the mysterious depths of the institute. Perhaps it's
> the blizzard raging outside, making the outdoors as threatening as anything you could imagine
> within. Perhaps it's the nightmare you had, hinting at horrific mysteries below and leaving you
> with **a strange object that seems to lead you inexorably downward**. Or perhaps it's just
> another way for you to avoid writing that **twenty page term paper you have due tomorrow**.
>
> In any event, you soon find yourself wandering away from your computer and into the dark nether
> regions of G.U.E. Tech. Suddenly, you're in a world that rivals your most hideous visions, a
> realm of horror lurking beneath the calm corridors and study halls. Shapes emerge from dark
> corners. Eerie sounds draw closer. Slimy passageways lead to sights so horrifying that they will
> feed your nightmares for weeks.
>
> THE LURKING HORROR recalls the ghastly visions of **H. P. Lovecraft and Stephen King**, as
> author Dave Lebling turns an everyday world into a frightening web of uncertainty. The numerous
> puzzles will challenge both first-time and experienced players, and Lebling's chilling
> descriptions will leave you with images you'll never forget.

Publisher's block: Infocom, 125 CambridgePark Drive, Cambridge, MA 02140; © 1987 Infocom, Inc.;
THE LURKING HORROR is a trademark of Infocom, Inc.; G-IH1-02. Screen shots are from the Macintosh
II, and the disk sold here is the Amiga 3.5".

So the whole game is one night in one building, with a storm outside and a deadline behind it —
**there is a reason to be down there, and it is not curiosity**, which matters if the person at
the keyboard starts asking why anyone would go into a basement full of monsters.

## The G.U.E. Tech student I.D. card

A laminated card in the box, printed both sides, and the most immediately useful object in the
package because it carries a number the game itself uses.

**The front** is headed **GEORGE UNDERWOOD EDWARDS INSTITUTE OF TECHNOLOGY — STUDENT I.D. CARD**,
with a barcode and the number **872325412**, the seal of the institute (a triangle, a pentagram, a
crescent, a closed eye, the date **1692**, and the motto **Omne ignotum pro magnifico est** —
"everything unknown is taken for magnificent"), and the small print:

> The individual whose signature and code appear on this card shall be entitled to all stated
> benefits of a G.U.E. Tech student, including the use of library and cafeteria facilities.
> Please present card when using said facilities.

**The back** is the standard student-identification boilerplate, printed in the flat voice of an
institution that has clearly had trouble before:

> This card is issued solely for your convenience. Do not allow anyone else to use it. Protect your
> card and code number at all times, and report any loss or theft promptly. Misuse of card,
> including bending, folding, spindling, or other mutilation, will result in loss of card
> privileges and other penalties as determined on an individual basis by the **G.U.E. Tech
> Discipline Committee**.
>
> This is not a credit card. This card remains the property of, and if found should be returned to,
> George Underwood Edwards Institute of Technology. It is not transferable, must be surrendered
> upon demand, and may be cancelled as well as repossessed and the privileges hereunder revoked at
> any time without prior notice.

…and a box at the bottom, blank: *SIGNATURE OF CARDHOLDER*.

## G.U.E. at a Glance — A Guide for Freshmen

A yellow-covered booklet, *"Presented as a public service by the G.U.E. Tech Social Committee"*,
and the real feelie here: it is a campus guide written by students, for students, in the margin
between officialese and gossip. **It is also a working document** — a freshman filled it in, and
the fillings-in are the interesting part.

### The filled-in pages

**IDENTIFICATION** — the form a student fills out on arrival, with the instructions that matter:

> Record your dormitory, advisor, and class schedule here, then carry this guidebook with you as a
> handy reference. **Your login number for the G.U.E. Technet computer system is the same as your
> student identification number.** You will also have a computer password, which should be kept
> secret at all times. It's best to memorize your password right away. **Never write it down where
> others might see it.**

Then *PERSONAL INFORMATION* (Name, Dorm/House, Dorm/House Phone, Advisor), *EMERGENCY
INFORMATION* (parent's name, address, phone) and a **CLASS SCHEDULE** grid — five days across,
seven-to-nine, nine-to-eleven, eleven-to-one, one-to-three and three-to-five down.

**The DIRECTORY page**, which is what the guide is for and what somebody has written in it:
*"don't forget snacks for Fri. party!"* across the top. Down the two columns, in a student's hand:

* **Chris Leu** — *Chapelgate*, phone *X 8990*
* *Chem lab T-TH 1-3*, *lab partner - Ardie Knowlton*, *X 2981*
* *Intro Calc* — *27-302*
* **Mitch** — *X 5417*
* …and, in the Password line of the last entry: **`UHLERSOTH`**, with the note **"don't tell
  anyone!!"** beside it, and *USER-ID 872325412* written along the bottom of the page.

That is the thing to know: **the guide the game ships contains a working password** — the same
number as the I.D. card, and the word `UHLERSOTH` written down where the guide itself says never to
write it. A player who had the box has it in their hands; a player without one has to find it
another way.

### Welcome to G.U.E

The opening spread carries a photograph of a columned portico with a student walking up the steps,
the institute's seal, and a quotation set in the margin:

> *He who climbs the steps of knowledge stands out among common men like a blueberry in a pan of
> milk.* — George Underwood Edwards

And then the guide's own welcome, which is the best description of the place anyone wrote:

> You've probably been waiting to go to G.U.E. Tech for years — ever since you realized that
> science and math were more important to you than just about anything (except eating). And now
> here you are, in a community of people who feel exactly the same way. Of course, the first thing
> in your mind is academics, whether you can get a seminar with that Nobel Laureate Physics
> professor, how soon you can have 24-hr access to the Computer Center, whether you can get credit
> through a work-study program…
>
> This handbook attempts to answer some of these questions. Written by upperclass students, *G.U.E.
> At A Glance* (sometimes known as **GAAG**) might tell you things the Administration would rather
> you didn't know. But we believe that you'd find them out anyway, and that you'll be glad it's
> sooner rather than later…
>
> In spite of what your roommate will tell you, **G.U.E. Tech does not have the highest suicide
> rate in the country.** However, it is a high-pressure school. While you're wondering what
> happened to the distraught student who used to sit next to you in Introductory Calculus, you
> might also be wondering how you're going to get through midterms without cracking up. When things
> get tough, **DON'T PANIC.** Help is always available, and no one will think the lesser of you for
> seeking it. Visits to the Counseling Center don't appear on your permanent record, and complete
> confidentiality is maintained at all times.

### School traditions

A boxed page, and every item of it is a possible thing to see in a college story:

* **Lock Day** — *"In a bizarre twist on Caltech's Ditch Day, seniors leave school, after locking
  the cafeteria doors with intricate electronic equipment. Underclassmen must undo the electronic
  puzzles before they can eat."*
* **Slug Stomping** — *"Watch for the first slugs of the year and step on them."*
* **Founder's Day** — *"One night during the last week of spring term, the huge bronze statue of
  George Underwood Edwards mysteriously disappears from its pedestal and shows up the next day in
  some totally offbeat spot. A certain rowdy fraternity is rumored to spend the entire year
  planning this prank."*
* **Final Scream** — *"At a designated time during Final Exam week, everyone screams in unison."*
* **Pigeon Day** — *"The President rings a bell at 6 a.m. one spring morning and puts a statue of a
  giant pigeon on the lawn. No classes for the day; free food at night."*
* **Streamer Day** — *"Take all the toilet paper rolls from the bathrooms and throw them out of the
  dorm windows."*

### The tunnels, and the guide's warning about them

Under a photograph of a long bare corridor — captioned *"The **Infinite Corridor** connecting the
Aeronautics and Chemistry buildings seems to stretch into infinity. It will soon become as familiar
to you as your dorm rooms."* — comes the passage that any player should read as instructions:

> Large, underground tunnels connect most of the buildings. However, many of these tunnels are very
> old, and a number have been closed for safety reasons. While you may be tempted to explore,
> **DON'T risk it. Several deaths have been attributed to student explorations in the tunnels.
> Closed tunnels are off-limits; they're closed for a reason, and we encourage students to restrain
> their curiosity and keep out of them.**

Which, in a horror game, is as close as the box comes to a map of where the story is going.

### The campus, and the map

A two-page spread prints the campus plan, in the style of a real freshman map: **Massachusetts
Avenue** on the west side, **Simth Street** across the north (the booklet's own typo), **Adams
Street** on the east, **River Street** along the south with the river drawn below it, and **Edwards
Square** at the north-east corner. The institute itself is the shaded complex in the middle: a
long **Aerospace** wing with **Engineering** at its foot, joined across the **Great Dome** to a
**Chemistry** wing with **Alchemy** at its foot, with the **Great Court** — a large triangle of
open space — between them. Around it: the **Computer Center** north-west, a **Temporary Building**
and **Nutrition** north-east, the **Brown Building** east of Chemistry, and a **Courtyard** to its
south. The caption records that the bequest of George Underwood Edwards "permitted the merger of
several venerable institutions, including the famed **Hopewell Academy of Natural Science**", that
departments were founded in "such radical disciplines as Mechanical Engineering, Chemical Biology,
and Architecture", and that *"although the campus has since expanded in all directions, nearly all
of the original structures remain."*

### Food, drink and where students go

* **FAVORITE HANGOUTS**, as the guide boxes them: **Crust and Noodles** — best Italian restaurant in
  town; **Reilly's Real Irish Pizza** — pizza delivery until 2 a.m.; **Hoagie Shack** — good subs,
  pastrami and sauerkraut a traditional favorite; **Rudy's Eating and Drinking Saloon** — "the local
  meat market"; **Dot n' Dash** — 24-hour diner, specializing in late night breakfast; **House of
  Roy** — in Chinatown, open late, great food and cheap.
* Men are advised to try **Rudy's** or the **Oak Haven Jr. College** Friday Night Mixer rather than
  the Capital Ave. bars; women are told there is no need to go anywhere, because *"with a male/
  female ratio of 6:1, someone WILL say hello to you."*
* Also recommended: the **Museum of Contemporary Art**, the **Looftier Aquarium**, the Freshman
  Drama Group, the Hellenic Club, writing a weekly column for the **G.U.E. NEWES**, and the
  **Advantage Athletic Complex** with its weekly paddleball tournaments. **Dave's Discount Decor**
  sells bean-bag chairs, lava lamps and designer telephones, including a favourite described as
  *"a glow-in-the-dark skull with gleaming red eyes, chortles instead of rings."*
* A class to take outside your requirements: **"The Zinc As Life Force"** seminar in the Department
  of **Alchemy**, which "makes full use of G.U.E.'s modern laboratory facilities."

### Some G.U.E. Tech jargon

The booklet's own glossary, and it is worth having because the game is set in a community that
talks like this:

* **frob** *(noun)* — a thing, useful when you have two "unspecified objects" on hand. *"Stick that
  frob on the thing over there."*
* **tool** *(noun)* — a nerd, someone who studies all the time and never takes time for a social
  life; *(verb)* to study. *"I'm tooling tonight."*
* **hack** *(noun)* — a prank. *"Painting the Dean's house pink was a great hack!"*; *(verb)* to
  commit a prank.
* **-p** *(suffix)* — adding it to a word makes the word a question, from LISP, where *p* means
  predicate: *"greaterp x y"* = "is x greater than y?". Most common among tool majors in Computer
  Science — *"Foodyp?"* ("food-pee") means *"Are you hungry?"*
* **grase** *(noun)* — student politician; *(verb)* to pass a course without working on it. *"I
  grased Thermodynamics."*
* **rug rat** (or just **rat**) *(noun)* — freshman.
* **urchin** *(noun)* — a local child or teenager who hangs around campus and often causes objects
  of value to mysteriously disappear.
* **curdle** *(verb)* — to astonish, usually unpleasantly. *"I was really curdled when I saw my
  final grades."*
* **flip** *(verb)* — to kill time by some essentially boring means, such as playing Hearts.
* **midnight** *(adjective)* — **before noon**. A "midnight class" is one held before noon; a
  "midnight quiz" is an early morning quiz.

And, in a paragraph of advice: the professors to beware of are **Prof. Hamstorp** (astronomy,
*"a tenured bore"*), **Prof. Tighe** (Intensive Metallurgy), **Dr. Morlock** (Introduction to Fuel
Gas Engineering), **Dr. Negele** (Moral Philosophy) and **Prof. Carlson** (a seminar in Textile
Technology) — *"and any junior instructor."*

### The alma mater

The last page prints the college song, *"Words and Music by Maj. Chas. Gleason, ret., '22"*, with a
note on pronunciation that is pure G.U.E.:

> *Note:* At the turn of the century, G.U.E. was commonly pronounced "gooey" (rather than
> "gee-you-ee" as it is today). The former pronunciation should be used when singing the alma
> mater.
>
> When Georgie planned his Institute / Upon the river's neck, / He little guessed the glories /
> That would fall on G.U.E. Tech.
>
> *(chorus)* So let's all toast our founder / Raise high the glass of beer / There's nothing we would
> rather be / Than a G.U.E. engineer.

…with the direction that follows it: *"It is customary for the chorus to be sung only by seniors and
alumni, while the tune is hummed by underclassmen. The final chorus is repeated, with everyone
joining in."*
