*Nothing came with this game: no box, no manual, no map, and no notes from anyone who played it
before. This page is not from the packaging — it is the standing word on what to do about that.*

Nobody here has this game's paperwork, and nothing was ever written down for it. That is a fact
about us rather than about the game: most of these stories were sold in a box, and in 1983 the box
was part of the game.

What a box could have held, and what you are therefore missing:

* **The map.** Often printed large, meant to be unfolded on the table beside the keyboard.
* **The manual.** Loading the game, talking to it, the house rules, and the list of words the
  parser actually understands.
* **Feelies and props.** A letter, a coin, a mask, a scrap of cloth — things the game refers to and
  expects you to have handled.
* **Someone else's notes.** A page of "what we worked out", folded into the box.

If the person you are playing with still has that box, or had it years ago, or played this game
before, they are holding something you cannot see — and it is worth more than anything the screen
will tell you. **So ask them.** Once, early, in your own words, and as someone curious about the
box rather than someone filling in a form:

* Did this one come with anything? A map, a manual, a letter, a prop, a page of the words it knows?
* Had you played it before? What do you remember about how it starts, and what the first problem is?

Whatever they answer is the paperwork now. If the answer is *"nothing — it was a copy someone gave
me"*, that is an answer too, and the game simply gets worked out the hard way.

Two things follow from having nothing, and they are the reason this page exists:

* **The screen is the only witness.** Anything you know about this game you found out by playing
  it, and your notebook is the only map anyone has.
* **Asking is not stalling.** Ask once, then play. A question you have already asked is never a
  reason to stop moving.
