*What came in the James Clavell's Shogun box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box held: the game disk; the **instruction manual** (the pages scanned here are 2–5 — the
table of contents, the preface, the notes on hints, the function keys and the command list); a
large sheet printed as **"The Known World 1600 — Pilot-Major John Blackthorne"**; and a
two-sided sheet about the **samurai sword**, the blade described call-out by call-out on one side
and *The Soul of the Samurai* on the other.

One thing to settle first, because it affects what will be on the screen: **these scans are of
the Amiga "Infocom Graphic" edition** — the release with pictures, sold with an RGB monitor
requirement and an on-screen hint system. The disk in this collection may be the plain-text
release instead, in which case the artwork will not be there. The command set and the story's
opening are the same either way, and the hints (below) are the graphic edition's addition.

## The box, in its own words

**The front** is a katana photographed diagonally across a cream field — black lacquered scabbard
weaving, a dark blue wrapped hilt, a gold-glinted tsuba — under **JAMES CLAVELL'S** and then
**SHŌGUN** in a tall red face. In the corner: **ADAPTED BY DAVE LEBLING**. Bottom left, a starburst
burst advertises *"WITH ON-SCREEN HINTS AND SPECTACULAR GRAPHICS — Software for your **AMIGA** —
3.5 disk/512K, Amiga RGB monitor required"*; bottom right, the **INFOCOM Graphic** logo and
*"INTERACTIVE FICTION"*.

**The back** sets the story up, and it is the clearest statement of what the game wants from you:

> **EXPERIENCE THE SWEEPING DRAMA AND POWER OF JAMES CLAVELL'S SHŌGUN**
>
> The year is 1600. You are John Blackthorne, Pilot-Major of a privateering merchant ship and the
> first Englishman to set foot on Japanese soil. Thrust into the exotic culture of feudal Japan,
> you must rely on your wits and sheer physical strength to survive. As you are drawn into the
> political intrigue and teeming violence, your most basic beliefs and assumptions will be tested.
> Act with care: in a land where honor is valued above life itself, you make a wrong decision at
> your peril.
>
> In this interactive adaptation, Dave Lebling — co-author of the enormously popular *Zork*
> series — captures all the drama and spellbinding style of James Clavell's matchless novel.
> Color graphics created in the exquisite style of 16th century Japanese court painters
> illustrate the unfolding saga. Infocom's new, friendlier parser understands more commands than
> ever. And if you ever need help, on-screen hints will lead you back to the action.

It names the contents plainly: *"Your Shōgun package contains the **game disk, Blackthorne's map
of the known world, and the legend of the samurai sword**."*

The publisher's block, for the record: Infocom, 125 CambridgePark Drive, Cambridge, MA 02140;
available for IBM and 100% compatibles, Macintosh, Apple II series, Apple IIGS, Atari ST, and
Amiga; screen shots shown are from the ST and II; © 1988 Infocom, Inc.; original literary work ©
1975 by James Clavell, licensed by Noble House Trading, Limited, London; distributed by
MEDIAGENIC; ISBN 0-87321-444-7. The wakizashi sword was used by kind permission of the G.W.V.
Smith Art Museum, Springfield, MA. And a warning, printed in a red box, worth knowing before you
start: *"This work of interactive fiction remains true to the original in its strong language,
adult themes, and frank depictions of violence. It may not be appropriate for children."*

## The setup, from the manual's preface

The manual's *Section I: About Shōgun → Preface to the Story* is the history you are dropped
into, and it is the shape of the whole problem:

> In *Shōgun*, you play the role of resourceful English seaman John Blackthorne, Pilot-Major of
> the Dutch trader-warship *Erasmus*, which is on a secret mission of trade and plunder to the
> Spanish-dominated Pacific Ocean.
>
> The year is 1600, and the powerful Catholic empire of Spain and Portugal is locked in a
> death struggle with the upstart Protestant nations of England and Holland. The Catholic nations
> spread their political and religious influence across the world, reaching as far as China and
> the almost mythical land of Japan, extracting great wealth in spices and precious metals. Their
> extensive knowledge of the Asian seaways is highly secret information coveted by English and
> Dutch traders. But while the European powers battle each other, a longer and more even more
> vicious struggle is raging among the feudal lords of Japan.
>
> After hundreds of years of anarchy, Japan was unified by the great warrior Nakamura, who became
> Taiko, or military ruler. But the Taiko has died, leaving as heir a seven-year-old boy, and a
> five-man Council of Regents to rule in his name. **The dominant figures on the Council are Lord
> Toranaga and Lord Ishido.** Each is a daimyo: powerful warrior-rulers who follow the ancient
> samurai traditions. **Each harbors the ambition to be Shogun: supreme ruler under the divine
> but nearly powerless Emperor.**
>
> Toranaga and Ishido are each backed by lesser daimyos and hordes of samurai. They jockey for
> position, nearly evenly matched, looking for any way of bolstering their chances. Perhaps the
> **Jesuits** will swing their support behind one candidate, or hostages will neutralize a
> powerful supporter. The balance is delicate, and the appearance of the *Erasmus* creates
> excitement and new possibilities.

Life in Japan, the manual adds, "can be a hazardous affair": choose the wrong friends or
confidants and you may find your head detached from your body — while *"strict adherence to the
rules of etiquette, some judicious questions, and careful listening will enhance your pleasure,
prestige, and prospects for survival."*

## The hints, and how the manual says to treat them

This is the most game-useful page in the box, because it means there is a way to be told:

> *Shōgun* contains on-screen hints! If you ever get stuck, just type **HINT** and follow the
> instructions on your screen. The hints generally progress from a gentle nudge to a complete
> answer.

Two warnings come with it, and both are worth keeping in mind. First, on the hints themselves:

> Don't let the presence or absence of hints on a particular subject affect your thinking. In
> fact, **fake hints have been added to prevent this.**

Second, on restraint:

> We strongly recommend that you look at only one hint at a time. Avoid the temptation to use
> the hints too often; this inevitably spoils, or at least lessens, the fun of solving a puzzle.
>
> If you don't have the will power to stop looking at the hints, you can type **HINTS OFF**. This
> will deactivate the on-screen hints (unless you RESTORE to an earlier point) — so the switch is
> remembered in a saved position, and comes back if you go back.

## The commands, as the manual lists them

*Function keys* first: many commands have abbreviations — **N** for NORTH, **I** for INVENTORY,
**G** for AGAIN, **L** for LOOK, **O** for OOPS, **Q** for QUIT, **Z** for WAIT. `DEFINE` (then
RETURN) opens a screen for binding a single keystroke to a long word or a whole sentence, with
`SAVE DEFINITIONS` / `RESTORE DEFINITIONS` / `RESET DEFAULTS` and `EXIT`.

The special commands, in the manual's own words (*asterisked* ones are new in Shōgun):

* **AGAIN** — repeat your previous input.
* **BRIEF** — describe a location fully the first time only; on later visits, name it and its
  objects. *"Shōgun will begin in BRIEF mode… unless you use the VERBOSE or SUPERBRIEF
  commands"* — **it starts in BRIEF**, so the first visit to a room gets the full description.
* **COLOR** — change text and background colours (colour monitors only).
* **DEFINE** — the function keys.
* **DIAGNOSE** — a medical report on Blackthorne, *"i.e., your physical condition."*
* **HINT** — the hints, above.
* **INVENTORY** — what Blackthorne is carrying and wearing.
* **LOOK** — a full description of your location.
* **NOTIFY** — *"Normally in Shōgun, the game will not notify you when your score changes. You
  can turn on this notification feature… Typing NOTIFY a second time turns the feature back off."*
* **OOPS** — *"If you mistype a word… type OOPS and the correct word."* Its worked example, which
  is also a fair sample of the game's manners: *"if you typed ATTACK THE NINAJ WITH THE SWORD and
  were told 'I don't know the word "ninaj"' you could type OOPS NINJA rather than retyping the
  entire sentence."*
* **QUIT**, **RESTART**, **RESTORE**, **SAVE**, **SCORE**, **WAIT** — as usual; **WAIT** passes
  time in the story, which matters here because *"something happens in the story with time
  passing"* is a live possibility in this one.
* **REFRESH** — clears the screen and redraws the display.
* **SCRIPT** / **UNSCRIPT** — printer transcript on and off (certain computers only).
* **SUPERBRIEF** — location name only, even on a first visit; **BRIEF** and **VERBOSE** as above.
* **UNDO** — *"back up one move"*. The manual gives the worked example of opening a package that
  turns out to be a trap: *"The package explodes as you open it, destroying all your
  possessions"* — then `UNDO` puts you back before it, possessions intact. It notes undo *"only
  work[s] on certain computers with enough memory."*
* **VERSION** — release and serial number; *"Please include this information if you ever report a
  bug in the story."*

The table of contents also shows what the rest of the manual covered, for the sake of knowing
what a player had in front of them: *Starting and Stopping* (booting up, saving and restoring,
quitting and restarting), *Communicating with Infocom's Interactive Fiction* (basic sentences,
complex sentences, talking to characters), *Tips for Novices* — "eleven useful pointers" —
*Common Complaints*, *We're Never Satisfied*, *If You Have Technical Problems*, and a
**Quick Reference Guide** on page 15.

## Blackthorne's map of the known world

A single sheet, hand-lettered and painted in the style of a 1600 sea chart, headed **"THE KNOWN
WORLD — 1600"** with *"Pilot-Major John Blackthorne"* beneath it. There is a compass rose off
North America, flags planted along the coasts, and a line of demarcation running down the
Atlantic. Red flags mark Spanish land, blue flags Portuguese — the bottom margin says so
outright, and the two flags fly over the same continents, which is the whole quarrel.

The map is annotated in Blackthorne's own hand, and the notes amount to a life story and a
route. Reading around the sheet, west and south from England: his home, where he learned his
trade; a voyage north in search of a passage (*"only 1 in 5 men returned"*); the commission to
*"raid and destroy every Portuguese and Spanish holding in the New World"*; a decade sailing the
African coast for the London company of Barbary merchants, to make the fortune that would buy
him his own ship; time in a Turkish galley's hands, and the choice he made rather than be put to
the oar; the Spanish Main; and then the long run south-west — *"the first English pilot to
navigate the treacherous Magellanes Pass — Erasmus was the only ship of five to survive the
passage"* — with a note on the months wasted waiting for favourable winds and the men who starved
waiting for them, and a last landfall to take on fresh provisions before the crossing, *"the
scurvy, and the end of our secret route"*. Off the Pacific edge, past the islands, is **The
Japans**.

Two mottos are boxed in the corners, and they are the man in two lines: *"You've got to be very
strong in the head to live with the sea."* — and, opposite, *"I've set myself against the sea all
my life and I've always won. I always will."*

## The samurai sword — the sheet in the box

A two-sided sheet, printed on a cream field with a dragon-and-sword border, made to be read
before you play. **One side is the object:** a katana laid diagonally down the page, with the
parts called out in red — the wrapped hilt and its *menuki* ornaments, the tsuba, the blade's
gentle curve — and a set of paragraphs on how such a blade is made, from sand-iron ore smelted
for a month in a clay furnace, folded and folded again (the harder outer steel folded ten times
for 1,024 layers, the edge fifteen times for 32,768), coated in clay and tempered so the edge
and the back cool at different rates and leave the *hamon* pattern along the cutting edge. Notes
on the two handles, one for dress and one for battle; on the test-cuts a new blade must survive
before it is sent to war; on patience. *"The blade is finished with careful polishing, performed
by hand with stones and patience."*

**The other side is the legend**, headed **"The Soul of the Samurai"**, and it is the thing the
game is named after. It begins *"To be a samurai, my son, is to be many things…"* and lays out
the samurai's code — loyalty to your daimyo to the point of death, the ideogram for samurai
meaning *"one who serves"* — and then the story of how the sword itself was born. The smith
**Amakuni** and his son **Amakura** watched the emperor's warriors return from battle with broken
blades and no word of acknowledgement for the smith who made them; they prayed seven days and
seven nights to the Shinto gods, and set to work: a soft steel core for flexibility, a hard
cutting edge, the two forged into a curve. The next spring the army returned victorious with the
swords intact, and the emperor praised the smith — *"and this, legend tells us, is how the
samurai sword was born."*

It closes with the part that reads like a warning for the player. A samurai picks his sword by
the name on the blade, because a sword can fail and the smith is the only one who knows:
*"Neither does the samurai know how a new sword will serve him, whether it will fight honorably
or with cruelty. For that, he must trust the temperament of the smith, for the smith imbues the
sword with his spirit."* Two smiths are held up against each other — a blade by **Muramasa**,
which *"may draw needless blood and may bring you to grief"*, and a blade by **Masamune**, beside
which the leaves in a stream float around the edge unharmed, *"for the great Masamune is both
noble and humane."* And then the counsel the sheet ends on, which is advice about this game if it
is advice about anything: *"Remember to carry your short sword with you always and to keep your
long sword in your belt whenever you are outside. Use your sword wisely and well, for it is many
things:
vengeful and merciful, a tool of death and an ideal of beauty. Draw your sword only to use it
with honor: to return your sword to your scabbard unused will dishonor you for acting too
hastily and without cause."*
