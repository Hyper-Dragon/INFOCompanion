*What came in the Bureaucracy box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box held: the bank's own moving booklet, **"You're Ready to Move"** (Fillmore Fiduciary
Trust, est. 1907); **three Fillmore Better Beezer card applications**, each printed differently;
the **invitation letter** to the Better Beezer card; a letter from the **Happitec Corporation**
about your new job; and an issue of **Popular Paranoia** magazine — quiz, advertising, subscription
offer and charter reservation form, all of it.

## About the release, and the form the game asks for

This is **release 160** — the copy-protected one. The story will not begin until its Licence
Application Form has been filled in, and everything it asks for is on the paperwork in this box.
What the bank wants is the **short form, 624Z87M-A** — that is where the chart on the flyer leads —
and the entries it wants are the ones already written on the sample form below, which is the whole
joke.

## "You're Ready to Move" — the bank's moving booklet

A glossy little booklet from **Fillmore Fiduciary Trust**, whose badge says *est. 1907* and whose
sign-off on every page is *"Consider us your friend... We make it EASY for YOU!"* It is a bank's
excuse to sell you things, a change-of-address manual, and a mildly unhinged questionnaire, all in
one.

- **Children have feelings, too.** A page about involving the children in the move. Take the
  family to the bank: opening a savings account for them costs a $10 minimum and earns a generous
  5% interest, "and your children will thrill to the excitement of sound money management". Ask at
  the **Change of Address window** and the teller will cheerfully hand you a form. The cartoons
  carry captions like *"Did you know you can use… CHANGE-OF-ADDRESS FORM, dear."* and *"Gee whiz,
  Dad, this is a BIG BANK!"*
- **Moving tips.** Wash and iron all curtains and wall hangings before packing; wrap all china and
  glassware in newspaper, in the special china cartons provided by your mover; pack essentials —
  toothbrushes, changes of clothes, the children's favourite toys — in a box that will be the last
  thing into the truck and the first thing out. Book **Mazzotta National Van Lines and
  Rent-a-Truck** well ahead so a van is reserved in your name, and file your change-of-address
  forms **six to eight weeks before you move**. On moving day your Mazzotta crew is "waiting
  outside your house, ready to transport your belongings with courtesy and efficiency".
- **Form 624Z87M (624Z87M-A): Instructions — "A Special Message to Our Customers."** The forms
  "have been completely revised to make it even easier for you to change your address", with "a
  simple question-and-answer format, illustrative examples of more complicated questions, sample
  worksheet space, color-coded step-by-step forms, and larger type". You may file whenever you
  change your primary residence, "with certain qualifications (explained later)". Peel the
  name-and-address label off the back cover and affix it in the FORMER NAME AND ADDRESS area — if
  the label is incorrect, do not use it. Then deliver the form to the bank in person: *"We regret
  that we are unable to accept forms sent through the mail."* You may be eligible to use
  **E-Z-Form 624Z87M-B** if you are moving to **Zalagasa** to start a new job — you must meet
  certain tests of time and distance; see your Customer Service Representative. Signed **L. M.
  Mazzotta, Chairman, Fillmore Fiduciary Trust, Inc.**
- **"MISTAKES DELAY YOUR BANK CORRESPONDENCE AND INCREASE SERVICE CHARGES. Only YOU can prevent
  mistakes."** The rules, as the booklet gives them: 1. Complete all items. 2. Check all facts.
  3. Sign where indicated; if married, both must sign; children over age 12 must file separate
  forms. 4. Use only blue ultra fine felt-tip pen. 5. Never fold, staple, bend, or mutilate this,
  or any other, form. (The form's own header asks for a black ballpoint pen; the line-by-line
  notes ask for a fountain pen with Permanent Blue-Black ink; the sample sheet asks for a #2
  pencil. Nobody coordinating these instructions has read all of them.)
- **"WHICH CHANGE OF ADDRESS FORM SHOULD YOU FILE?"** A flow chart in pink and red. Its questions,
  in its own order: Is your new home in the U.S.? Are you moving within 10 miles of your old home?
  Are you renting or buying your new home? Did you obtain your mortgage through Fillmore
  Fiduciary Trust? Was your mortgage over $100,000? Do you plan to obtain a 2nd mortgage through
  Fillmore Fiduciary Trust? Are you relocating as a result of an atomic war or other nuclear
  disaster? Is the difference between your new zip code and your old zip code odd or even? How
  many times have you moved within the past year? Have you made a bank deposit within the last 5
  days? Have you made a bank withdrawal within the last 5 days? Do you own a luxury car, a
  vacation home, a VCR, or a wide-screen TV; and, if so, are you willing to donate any or all of
  these properties to the President of Fillmore Fiduciary Trust? Are you moving in with your
  in-laws? Did you eat breakfast this morning? The chart ends at one of two boxes — "YOU MUST FILE
  LONG FORM 624Z87M" or **"YOU MUST FILE SHORT FORM 624Z87M-A"** — and the answer the game is
  after, when it asks, is the short form.
- **When should you file?** At least two months, but no sooner than six weeks, before your moving
  date; ask for **Pamphlet 96, "Penalties and Interest on Forwarding Expenses Due to Late Filing of
  Change-of-Address Form."** If a customer dies before filing, the spouse or personal
  representative must file. A widow or widower who has not remarried may file jointly by writing
  *"Filing as Surviving Spouse"* in the Signature of Spouse section, showing the date of death in
  the Name of Spouse space, and attaching a notarised copy of the death certificate — see
  **Pamphlet 974, "Banking Protocol for Survivors, Executors, and Morticians."** The lines are
  open Monday to Friday, 10:00 a.m. to 2:00 p.m.; *"We regret that we are unable to accept phone
  calls."*
- **The line-by-line notes**, with their worked examples. Willamena Steere eats nothing but a
  chocolate-covered donut every morning, so her diet does not meet FDA minimum nutrition
  requirements, and because April, May and June are all given names she must postpone her move to
  Hershey, Pennsylvania until July 1987 — see **Pamphlet 482, "Is That a Baby or a Month?"**
  Rupert Swarm's story works out to "December 12, 1986" on Line 17. Mary Brownell, left a
  multimillion-dollar trust fund by her great aunt, should check the "Trust Fund" box on Line 20.
  Line 23: if you are moving in with your in-laws, mother-in-law must sign too.

## The three Fillmore Better Beezer card applications

Three near-identical application blanks, each printed with its own nonsense, each to be filled in
with the pencil the bank provided — bear down hard, *"as you will be making three copies. Do not
separate the copies."*

1. **The first** (the top copy): *"Answer questions 1-20 using the #2 pencil provided. Do NOT
   write in shaded areas. Answer questions 1-16 only."* It asks for last name, date of birth
   (day/year/month), age in months, ZIP, present and previous address, number of children
   including spouse, bank name and account number, type of account (checking / savings / other),
   and one of: own home / rent / own condo-co-op / other. One question asks what your spouse and
   friend do: make love / hate me / like to polka. Its signature paragraph authorises Beezer
   International to check your credit history and permanent record, and to exchange information
   about you "with credit bureaus and institutions doing business in North America, Europe, Asia,
   South America, Australia, Africa, Antarctica, Guam, or elsewhere, or with any person or persons
   that Beezer International may so choose… without recourse"; it absolves the bank of any
   misrepresentations of fact, agrees that you will pay for any charges billed to your account "by
   Beezer International or its employees", and notes that failure to answer questions 4 and 12
   invalidates the application.
2. **The second**: *"using the black pen provided. Write ONLY in the shaded areas. Answer
   questions 1-15 only."* It asks for first name, today's date, annual income, previous city,
   previous address, mailing address, your intelligence quotient, spouse, best friend and the age
   of the friend, the same make-love / hate-me / polka line, and check-one: own home / **application
   denied**. This one's paragraph — headed *"PLEASE TAKE RESPONSIBILITY"* — authorises Beezer to
   **alter** your credit history and permanent record; forbids using the card to purchase "drugs,
   alcohol, cigarettes, clothes, food, paper, plastics, textiles, cheese, popcorn poppers,
   services, or products of any kind"; says use of the card authorises Beezer to cancel your
   rights as a cardholder; sets a $20 per month service fee which "may be increased by any amount
   at any time"; requires you to report any change in your name, address, employer, weight, sex,
   salary, hair color, or shirt; and states that failure to answer questions 4 and 12 is a
   violation of the law.
3. **The third**: *"Answer questions 1-20 using the Burg scoring system. Erase shaded areas.
   Answer questions 1-13 only."* It asks for full name, spouse's name, hat size / shoe size / ring
   size, favourite colour, number of tattoos, nearest toxic waste dump ("we're just curious"), the
   names of your children, the highest number you can think of, where you were last fired from,
   pancakes eaten, car make, accidents in 1987, and type of accidents (head-on / sideswipes /
   fender-benders); its check-one list includes "I drink heavily", "I chew with my mouth open", "I
   wear ratty underwear" and "I enjoy sheep". Its paragraph is headed **"TAKE A FLYING LEAP"**: you
   swear the above is true and authorise Beezer to change it and swear the changes are true too;
   Beezer may sign any documents in your name; you will hold Beezer "totally blameless" for any
   misfortunes; Beezer may use your endorsement in advertising or in the courtroom; and failure to
   answer questions 4 and 12 is this time punishable by death.

## Filling the form in, when the game asks

The Licence Application Form takes **one character at a time** and will not say which character it
disliked: a low-pitched beep means "not that one" and the field is redrawn. The fields are narrow —
measured, the name field took eleven characters and beeped at everything after them — so type what
fits and let the extras be refused rather than fighting them. The two entries that used to be hard
to read off the handwriting are now settled from a closer look at the sheet: the totals line reads
**28,3974** (4 + 24 = 28, 1987 + 1987 = 3974 — the sample adds its dates as strings), and the
dental figure is **$8,748**. If a field still will not take what is written above, say so and ask
rather than guess. And if none of it works, this game cannot be started: say so plainly, offer the
others, and do not spend the evening beeping at a form.

## How these papers are meant to be used

Every piece of this is the sort of mail a person's life actually arrives in: a bank selling you a
card in the guise of a moving guide, an employer's welcome letter, a magazine you would have to
write away for — and one thin, dull form at the centre of it that the world insists on having
filled in. The bank wants the form; the sample shows how the bank fills it in; the chart shows
which one to file. Nothing here is a walkthrough — the world of Bureaucracy is yours to navigate,
and this is the paperwork you carry into it.
