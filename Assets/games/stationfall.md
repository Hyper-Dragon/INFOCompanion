*What came in the Stationfall box, transcribed from the package itself.*

**The instruction manual was not among these scans**, and neither was the back of the box, the disk or its
sleeve — the set is the box front, the manila envelope of **Stellar Patrol Official Blueprints** with its
nine drawing sheets and key, three printed green-bar forms, and one embroidered cloth patch.

The box says what the game is in a single line across its face: *"FLOYD IS BACK IN THE BOFFOID SEQUEL TO
PLANETFALL."* Everything else in the package is bureaucratic: you are a **Lieutenant First Class** of the
**Stellar Patrol of the Third Galactic Union**, issued with orders, authorizations and a station's worth
of engineering drawings. The joke of the feelies is that the paperwork is real, dense and internally
consistent — a player holding this box had a deck plan of the place they were about to walk around in.

## What the scans show, and what they do not

Sixteen images, all listed. Ten are the blueprints (an envelope cover, a key sheet, and nine numbered
drawing sheets); three are the forms; two are box art; one is the patch.

No scan shows a platform line, a disk format, an ISBN, a publisher address or a copyright date. The box
front is trimmed to the artwork, so the only publishing evidence anywhere in the set is the **™** beside
the title and the part codes carried by the feelies, which all take the form **`G-IS6-…`**:
`G-IS6-ENV` on the envelope, `G-IS6-1BLUE` on the key, `G-IS6-2BLUE` through `G-IS6-10BLUE` on the nine
sheets, and `G-IS6-FORM1`, `-FORM2`, `-FORM3` on the forms. Which machine this box was sold for cannot be
determined from these scans.

## The box front

Yellow letters across the top — **STATIONFALL**, with a small **™** at the upper right — and beneath them,
in the same yellow, the tagline *"FLOYD IS BACK IN THE BOFFOID SEQUEL TO PLANETFALL."*

The rest is one illustration. The ground behind the figure is a tan field ruled with thin red horizontal
lines, as though the whole cover were printed on one of the Patrol's own forms; into that field is set a
large dark triangle, full of stars, and inside the triangle hang three pale violet-and-pink **ringed
spheres** — one large, two smaller.

In front of them runs a figure in a **red helmet** bearing a gold roundel on the crown, a dark blue
visor, and a **green flight suit** with a bright orange-yellow collar, a small round patch on the left
chest, dark gloves, a heavy harness strap, and chunky violet-and-black boots with a pink trim. He is
mid-stride, carrying an armful of **yellow printed sheets** — forms — and more sheets fly loose past him
and lie scattered on the ground to the right, one of them carrying lines of print far too small to read.
No Infocom mark, no copyright line and no company address is visible anywhere on the scan.

## The cloth patch

A circular embroidered patch, gold border on a red ground, and effectively the same emblem as the
envelope: the triangle, **BOLDLY GOING** down one edge and **WHERE ANGELS** down the other, the gold-ringed

## How these papers are meant to be used

You are the Lieutenant First Class named on the forms, and the paperwork is your errand: a spacetruck
from the Deck Five cargo bay, **24 pallets of Request Form Forms** waiting at Space Station Gamma Delta
Gamma. The blueprints are the station you are about to board — the kind of drawing a courier is handed at
the airlock and expected to read in a corridor — and between the key sheet, the side-view cutaway, the
seven deck plans and the two level-odd and level-even sheets, every deck, every door type, every barracks
cell and every connection point for a sub-module the station has is drawn somewhere in the set. Which
sheet matters, what a course figure of 103 means, and what the *"Top-Priority assignment"* turns into,
the box does not say.
