*What came in the Sherlock box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box lists its contents in a photograph on the back, arranged on a leather case with a
deerstalker, a magnifying glass and a glove: *"Regularly arrayed inside every The Riddle of the
Crown Jewels package: a tourist map of Victorian London; a copy of the June 17, 1887 'London
Thames'; and a distinctive key fob."* All three are in the scans — the map, the **newspaper in
full (eight pages)**, and the fob. The instruction manual was not among them.

## The box, in its own words

**The front**: **SHERLOCK** in large red letters on a pale patterned ground, *The Riddle of the
Crown Jewels* in a panel beneath, and inside an oval a painted London — the Houses of Parliament
and the Clock Tower behind, a man in a bowler hat and moustache on the left (Watson), Sherlock
Holmes on the right in deerstalker and cape with his pipe, and below them, in shadow, the long
face of a third man who is not introduced. In the corner: the **INFOCOM IMMORTAL LEGENDS** roundel,
*"Interactive Fiction by Bob Bates"*, *"With OnScreen Hints"*, and *"SOFTWARE FOR YOUR AMIGA
(3½" DISK)"*.

**The back** is the brief — and it is the whole premise, so it is worth having entire:

> **MORIARTY HAS A DEADLY TRAP FOR SHERLOCK HOLMES. AND ONLY YOU CAN STOP HIM…**
>
> Travel back in time to Victorian London, where the city is bustling with preparations for Her
> Majesty's **Golden Jubilee**. Crowds of sight-seers and souvenir vendors fill the streets in
> eager anticipation of the Jubilee Week events. Newspapers detail the gala array of festivities.
> Sumptuous receptions for foreign dignitaries. Special services at Westminster Abbey. A Royal
> Procession through the streets of London. And the Queen reigning over all, resplendent in the
> Crown Jewels.
>
> At least, that's the official plan.
>
> Unbeknownst to the celebrants thronging the city, a crisis has arisen: **the Crown Jewels have
> been stolen from the Tower of London.** If they're not recovered before the festivities begin,
> the theft will be exposed and the government will fall into international disgrace.
>
> **Only 48 hours remain to solve the crime.** With Scotland Yard failing to make headway, the
> Prime Minister calls on Sherlock Holmes, the famous consulting detective. But riddles left at the
> scene of the crime include a direct challenge to Holmes, who suspects a deadly trap. **To throw
> the scoundrel off his guard, Holmes turns the investigation over to you, his trusted cohort, Dr.
> Watson.**
>
> With Holmes by your side, you use your wits, intuition, and a myriad of clues to solve the
> riddles and piece together the mystery. Your search for the jewels and the villain leads you all
> over London, from the most popular tourist attractions to the seediest back alleys. **As Big Ben
> strikes each successive hour** and dangerous complications impede your progress, you realize
> you're facing that most dastardly of foes, Holmes's archenemy… **the vile Professor Moriarty.**
>
> *Sherlock: The Riddle of the Crown Jewels* is the first story in Infocom's new **Immortal Legends**
> series, developed by **Challenge, Inc.** … **On-screen hints** provide clues when your magnifying
> glass falls short of the task.
>
> And now, come, Watson! The game is afoot…

The practical facts in that paragraph are the ones to hold on to: **you are Watson, not Holmes**;
Holmes is *with* you; there is a **48-hour clock**; and there is a hint system on screen.

## The key fob

A small rubber fob on a bead chain, moulded in relief: two men in profile inside a heart-shaped
outline, one with a bowler and moustache, one with a deerstalker and pipe — Holmes and Watson, in
other words — with **SHERLOCK** and the Infocom mark beneath them. It came in the box, it has no
part in the game, and it is the sort of thing that is useful to know about if the person at the
keyboard mentions having one.

## The newspaper: *The Thames*, Friday, June 17, 1887

A complete Victorian newspaper, printed on period paper: **No. 32,101**, **LONDON, FRIDAY, JUNE 17,
1887**, **PRICE 3d.**, with a small engraved crest (*Verum intus*, "the truth within") and *Situs
est* over a woodcut of a country house. It runs to eight pages across four broadsheet sections:
**BIRTHS, MARRIAGES, DEATHS** and *In Memoriam*; **PERSONAL &c.**; **MISCELLANEOUS**; **ART
EXHIBITIONS**; **ENTERTAINMENTS**; **HOTELS &c.**; **SHIPPING**; **HORSES, CARRIAGES, &c.**;
**SITUATIONS**; **FOREIGN INTELLIGENCE**; **COURT CIRCULAR**; **THE QUEEN'S JUBILEE**;
**PRODUCE MARKET**; **THE WEATHER** (with a hand-drawn isobar chart); **WEEKEND ALMANAC** (tide
tables); and **SPORTING INTELLIGENCE**.

The date is not decoration — it is the game's clock. The paper is the **Friday**; the Jubilee is
the following **Tuesday, June 21st**, which several advertisements name outright (*"TUESDAY, June
21st, being Her Majesty's Jubilee"*, and *"PHILLIPS … ILLUMINATIONS for June 21st"*), and the
Jubilee procession is described in the Court Circular. Everything below is from the issue.

### The notice that matters most

Under **PERSONAL &c.**, the first item on the page:

> **TOWER OF LONDON.** — Notice is hereby given that the **TOWER WILL BE CLOSED FOR THE PUBLIC**,
> from the Friday, the 17th, to the Saturday, the 25th inst., both inclusive, **due to increased
> need for security on the occasion of Her Majesty's Jubilee.**

A player reading the box has just been told the Crown Jewels were stolen from the Tower. The
newspaper they were given, dated the day the news breaks, closes the Tower to the public until
after the Jubilee — **for security**, it says. That is the cover story, printed on the prop in
the player's hands, and it is the first real contradiction in the case.

### The Jubilee, as the paper reports it

* **THE QUEEN'S JUBILEE.** *"It has now been decided that the Princess of Wales shall go down to
  Buckingham Palace with her daughters in an open carriage drawn by four horses, followed by two
  carriages containing the household suite of the Princess of Wales. At the Palace the Princess of
  Wales will alight and will subsequently enter the Queen's carriage, where she will sit during the
  procession facing her Majesty, the Crown Princess of Germany being seated by the Queen's side…
  the Princess of Wales will wear the military uniform of their respective corps, the Prince of
  Wales being in the uniform of a general."* General **Greys, G.C.B.**, commanding the Home
  District, has settled the arrangements for "the great military and naval display to be made next
  Tuesday".
* **The procession route, from the advertisements**: Admiral Beaton's tours (below) and three
  separate agents selling **grandstand seats for the Royal Jubilee Procession** — Mr. Collivier in
  Penton-street, R. Waterlooe-place in Hammersmith, and Fortunum, Mason and Co., whose notice reads
  *"Jubilee Procession. — FORTNUM, MASON and Co. beg to inform their numerous friends that all the
  vacant SPACE in their WAREHOUSE is already ALLOTTED"*.
* **In the Court Circular**: the Queen has been pleased to command that the members of the House of
  Houses sit together for the Thanksgiving Service at Westminster Abbey; *"that clerks and officers
  of the House… the Queen's Counsel… shall be permitted to accompany the members of the House"*; and
  the illuminations of houses in Grosvenor-place, Cornwall, Berkeley-square and Belgrave-square,
  *"the residences of Mrs. W. H. Smith, the Duke of Northumberland, Sir Edward Guinness, and the
  Wellington Club, will be lit up on Monday and Tuesday evenings."*
* **ROYAL VICTORIAN PATRIOTIC ASYLUM FOR GIRLS** — the Princess of Wales distributed the prizes, in
  connexion with the Royal Commission Patriotic Fund; 263 children in the school; the Princess
  arrived at 4.30; the children sang the National Anthem, after which the Princess presented the
  prizes to the 54 winners.
* And, usefully for anyone moving around the city on the day: **the Commissioner of Police for the
  Metropolis requests that the foot passengers… will pass in two streams — left hand to left hand
  and right hand to right hand.** *"The police will be stationed at the corners of the streets…
  carriages in the line are requested to proceed leisurely down the hill."*

### The rest of the news, in short

* **FOREIGN INTELLIGENCE** (Leipzig, 16/6/87) — the **treason trial** against the procurator-general
  demanded sentences of two to three years' hard labour in the case of four of the accused, several
  years' detention for two others, and acquittal for the remaining two.
* **FIRE IN ROUMANIA** (Bucharest, 16/6/87) — a destructive fire in the northern part of the town;
  *"the official report states that 800 houses were destroyed and seven people killed."*
* **ABDUCTION OF A LADY** (Paris, 16/6/87) — the Marquise Martinez carried off in broad daylight
  from the Bois de Boulogne by some persons in a carriage; the Spanish ambassador has been asked
  for assistance; no trace of the missing lady or her kidnappers, and the abduction is attributed
  to a French gentleman who was an unsuccessful suitor for her hand.
* **THE CRIMES BILL** (Concord, 16/6/87) — the House of Representatives has passed a resolution
  condemning the Irish Crimes Bill and congratulating Mr. Gladstone and Mr. Parnell.
* **JUBILEE TREAT** (Paris, 16/6/87) — 800 young children, orphans chiefly, with their mothers, had
  "a jubilee treat" to-day at Acheres, in the Forest of St. Germain; **Bismarck** is reported to be
  indisposed, and the Emperor and Chancellor may meet at Kissingen on 12th August; *"the Prince
  Bismarck doubled himself against the bad weather by driving to Kissingen."*
* **SHORT TIME IN THE COTTON TRADE** — the meeting of the cotton spinners and manufacturers of
  Lancashire to be held in Manchester, "the employers in several districts are so far agreed".
* **SPORTING INTELLIGENCE** — Cambridge University v. Sussex: *"The Cambridge Eleven of 1887 will
  have occasional claims for distinction. Yesterday they were in the field the whole day, and
  managed to score against them at the rate of between 70 and 80 an hour."*
* **PRODUCE MARKET** — tea, spices, shellac, green fruits, the Emu oil market, and a note that
  Messrs. Michael & Co. report the tea market "slow, but prices fully maintained" and that the
  long-tinned teas this week totaled about 11,959 bales.
* The **weather page** prints a chart of isobars for 16 June with an "explanation of the chart" —
  *"the dotted lines are 'isobars' or lines of equal barometrical pressure"* — and the state of the
  sea is noted in capital letters for each coast: 1 fresh, 2 smooth, etc. The weekend almanac gives
  the tides at Dublin and Liverpool for Saturday and Sunday.
* **Half a column of the paper is Stanley's letters from the Congo** — Henry M. Stanley, at Camp
  Leopoldville, Stanley Pool, April 26: the abject state of the Congo Independent State, the famine
  and the flooded river, *"the State, the English missions and traders are in the same difficulties
  as we are. You cannot imagine how great the stress is here"*, with the full correspondence between
  Stanley, Major Barttelot, Mr. Mounteney Jephson and Mr. Bonny of the Livingstone Inland Mission
  about the steamer *Stanley* — a genuinely long slice of period journalism and a reminder that
  this paper was not written as a puzzle, it was cut and pasted from the real thing.

### The advertisements

The classified columns are where a Victorian newspaper lives, and they are worth reading for the
period voice as much as anything. A sample: a report that **"A., COME HOME. All is forgiven. —
Vicki."**; a two-pound reward for lost golden daffodils, *"Return to W. Wldswith. No questions
asked."*; a **change of name** — *"JAMES CHISHOLM GOODEN … with the license of the Lord Lyon King
of Arms, dated the 23rd day of May, 1887, have ASSUMED and intend henceforth to use the NAME of
JAMES CHISHOLM GOODEN-CHISHOLM."*; a caution from Messrs. Merryweather and Sons of Long-acre
against *"spurious and worthless imitations of their own 'MERRYWEATHER' and 'HOSE'"*; **Carter's
Little Liver Pills** (*"Carter's Little Liver Pills move the cause of bad breath"*); **Liebig
Company's Extract of Meat**; **Lamplough's Pyritic Saline**; **Phillips' illuminations** for the
21st June; and, in the personal columns, advertisements for governesses, a "lady to join her" on
the tenth annual tour as a travelling companion, a full-sized Venetian gondola for sale, and
*"MASSAGE, in skilled hands, invaluable"* for the treatment of neuralgia and nervous exhaustion.

## Admiral Beaton's London Tours — the map

A large paper map of central London in the period manner, plate-printed with the river, the parks,
the principal buildings and the maze of streets, and overprinted in **orange with a tour route**.
In the corner is the agency's own advertisement, and it is what makes the map a prop rather than a
plan:

> **Authorized by Her Majesty the Queen**
> **Admiral Beaton's**
> **LONDON TOURS**
> *THE BEST AND FINEST TO BE FOUND*
>
> Jubilee Week tours include all regular London sights, with grandstand seats for the Royal
> Jubilee Procession.
>
> Daily Tours … 10s. 6d.
> 5-day Jubilee Tour including grandstand seat … 6 gns.
> Reservations taken. Early application suggested for reservations.
>
> **ADMIRAL BEATON'S LONDON TOURS**, 18, Cromwell place, S.W. (SEE MAP)

The advertisement is illustrated with a mounted guardsman in a busby, and the same agency's notice
appears in the newspaper's classified columns, which is the sort of consistency that makes a
period prop feel real.

**What the map shows**, west to east: **Hyde Park**, **Kensington Gardens** and **Hyde Park
Corner**; **Regent's Park**, **London University College**, **Russell Square** and the **British
Museum**; **Gray's Inn**; the **Post Office**; **Euston**, **King's Cross** and the **Liverpool
Street** stations; **Buckingham Palace** with its gardens, **Green Park**, **St James's Park**,
the **Royal Academy** and the **National Gallery** around **Trafalgar Square**; **Charing Cross**,
**Covent Garden** and **Scotland Yard**; **Westminster Bridge**, **Westminster Abbey**, the
**Houses of Parliament** and **Lambeth Bridge**; **Waterloo Station** and the **Archbishop's
Park**; the **Bethlehem Lunatic Asylum**; **Guildhall**, **St Paul's Cathedral**, the **Royal
Exchange**, the **Bank**, **Cheapside** and **Cornhill** in the City; **Leadenhall Street**,
**Aldgate** and the **Monument** to the east; and, at the far east, **the Tower of London**, on the
river. The **Thames** is the spine of it, with the bridges named.

Two things follow from the map for anyone playing this one: **London here is a set of rooms you
walk between** — the game's geography is this sheet, so the sheet is a genuine artefact of the
puzzle rather than illustration — and **the orange tour route is a period-dressed hint** about
where a sightseeing party would go.

## Corroboration, and what the props agree on

The box, the map and the newspaper were made to agree with each other, and the agreement is the
case against Moriarty. All three name the **Queen's Jubilee**; the newspaper dates it to **Tuesday,
21 June 1887**; the newspaper closes the **Tower** from the **17th to the 25th** and gives
*"security"* as the reason; the box says the **Crown Jewels** have been taken from the Tower and
gives **48 hours** to find them; and the map advertises **grandstand seats for the procession**,
which is where the story will end. A player who reads the paperwork before typing has been handed
the crime, the deadline, the alibi and the map — which is exactly what a box in 1988 was for.
