*What came in the Leather Goddesses of Phobos box, transcribed from the package itself.*

These scans show the box front; the 3-D glasses; the scratch 'n' sniff card; the two "Secret Catacombs
Map" sheets; and the comic book *The Adventures of Lane Mastodon* — issue 91 — which carries the
game's instruction manual bound in at the back, starting on its page 12. **The instruction manual is
therefore present**, printed inside the comic rather than as a separate booklet. Not among the scans:
the game disk, the "separate Reference Card" the manual repeatedly sends you to for booting, saving
and restoring, the hint-booklet and map order form the manual mentions, and the back of the box.

Edition facts visible in the scans: the manual's colophon carries **© 1986 Infocom, Inc.** and
**Printed in U.S.A.**, with the publisher at **Infocom, Inc., 125 CambridgePark Drive, Cambridge,
MA 02140**. *Leather Goddesses of Phobos* and *A Mind Forever Voyaging* are credited there as
trademarks of Infocom, Inc., *Planetfall* and *Sorcerer* as registered trademarks. No ISBN, platform
line or disk-format line appears anywhere in these scans, so the machine and version cannot be
identified from them. Each manual page carries a flat pink band across its top edge in the scan.

## The box front

The front is a starfield: black space crowded with white stars and streaks, a small purple cratered
moon and a slim rocket at the left, a large pale planet rising behind the title, and, across the
lower right, a green cratered landscape carrying a white domed city with towers and a central spire.
The title — **LEATHER GODDESSES OF PHOBOS**, with a small ™ — is lettered in heavy yellow capitals
on a rising diagonal. Two badges are printed on the art, and they are the whole of the box's sales
copy. On the lower left:

> WITH ALLURING 3-D AND SCENT-SATIONAL SCRATCH ~ SNIFF

and on the lower right, in a red starburst over three small banners:

> 3 EXCITING PLAYING MODES — TAME / SUGGESTIVE / LEWD

That is all the front says. There is no blurb, no screenshot, no author credit, no publisher or
platform line, and no price printed on it.

## The 3-D glasses

A pair of die-cut cardboard spectacles in white card, with a folding tab for the ears and rounded
frames. The wearer's left lens is blue and the right lens red, both squares of saturated colour, each
with a faint diagonal score across it. The scan shows one side only, with no text printed on it.

No printed instructions for the glasses are among the scans — only the box's "WITH ALLURING 3-D"
badge, the comic cover's "IN THRILLING 3-D!!" starburst, and the interior credit line naming the 3-D
process as **RAY ZONE**.

## The scratch 'n' sniff card

A single white card, framed by a heavy black art-deco border, with the game's title centred at the
top:

> LEATHER GODDESSES of PHOBOS™

Seven circular scratch spots are laid out in two rows, three across the top and four across the
bottom, each printed with a dotted numeral and its own colour:

* **1** — orange
* **2** — yellow
* **3** — purple
* **4** — green
* **5** — orange-red
* **6** — indigo

The card carries one instruction, the only printed guidance for the feature anywhere in the scans:

> scratch 'n' sniff until the story tells you to do so.

The manual prints one such prompt later on, in its own sample transcript: a smell is described and
the reader is told to scratch a spot and then press RETURN. The instruction printed there names
spot number nine, which does not match the seven spots on this card; both readings are as printed.

## The two Secret Catacombs Maps

Two sheets of cream stock printed in a single brownish-red ink, each with a thin border rule and a
title centred at the foot. They are hand-drawn line plans of a tunnel network with no place names and
no room labels: corridors run straight between small circles (junctions), with rounded rectangular
loops and two or three plain squares at corridor ends, and small chevrons mark the passages that
change level or run one way.

**Map 1 — "Secret Catacombs Map / Very Lower Level."** No compass rose.

**Map 2 — "Secret Catacombs Map / Lower Level."** The same drawing style, with a skewed compass
arrow at the lower left whose barbs run up and to the right, labelled **N**.

Neither map carries any printed instruction, warning or key; the only text on either sheet is its
two-line title.

## The instruction manual, bound into the comic

Pages 12 to 24 of the same book are the manual; its page numbers run 12 to 24, and its pink-paper
band shows at the top of every page in the scans.

### The manual's title page and table of contents

The title page is headed **Instruction Manual for Leather Goddesses of Phobos**, with a note on how
much of it to read:

> If you've never played Infocom's interactive fiction before, you should read this entire
> instruction manual.
>
> If you're an experienced Infocom player, just read *Section I: About Leather Goddesses of Phobos*.

The contents are set out in two parts. *Section I: About Leather Goddesses of Phobos* gives Preface
to the Story 13, Some Recognized Verbs 13, The Three Levels of Naughtiness 13, Sample Transcript and

### Preface to the Story

The manual sets the game in 1936 in the flat voice of a newsreel, and then explains what the player
is doing there:

> 1. In the United States, a new miracle fabric called nylon was becoming popular, Alf Landon was
> running for President, Victor Hess had won the Nobel Prize for Physics for discovering cosmic
> radiation, *Gone With the Wind* was the best-selling novel, and steak cost twenty-five cents a
> pound. Elsewhere, black track star Jesse Owens was embarrassing Adolph Hitler by winning four gold
> medals at the Berlin Olympics, Edward the VIII of England was abdicating his throne to marry a
> commoner, the Spanish Civil War was beginning to heat up, and the Leather Goddesses of Phobos were
> preparing to invade the Earth and turn it into their private pleasure world.
>
> Don't let anyone tell you that nothing ever happens in Upper Sandusky, Ohio, because on this day
> in 1936, you're snatched out of your favorite bar — kidnapped by minions of the evil Leather
> Goddesses. You are brought back to Phobos as an experimental subject, as preparations continue to
> enslave every man and woman on Earth.
>
> If you succeed in escaping the clutches of the Leather Goddesses, you will begin an odyssey the
> likes of which you have never even imagined (except, perhaps, in certain very enjoyable dreams).
> With your loyal friend and fellow Earthmate at your side, you will begin a naughty, bawdy, rowdy,
> rousing and very, very amusing romp across the solar system. Your mission, should you be able to
> catch your breath long enough to think about it, is to collect the materials you'll need to
> ultimately defeat the Leather Goddesses of Phobos and save humanity! Are you "up" for the job?

The manual notes in the sample transcript's introduction that in this game you play "either a male or
a female character" — the only place the scans say so.

### The Three Levels of Naughtiness

This is the printed explanation of the switch the box front advertises, and the manual sets out
exactly what each level contains:

> You can select from among three levels of naughtiness. The first is the TAME level, which has
> absolutely no "bad" language or sex-related situations. Next is the SUGGESTIVE level, which
> includes some naughty language and sexual innuendo, but nothing that you wouldn't see on network
> TV. Finally, if you're feeling truly risqué, there's our LEWD level, which includes most of George
> Carlin's Seven Words You Can't Say on Television as well as examples of almost graphic sex.
> Naturally, most people find the LEWD level the most fun. You can roughly equate these three levels
> with the G, PG, and R movie ratings. The story will always begin in the SUGGESTIVE level. The
> STATUS command will tell you your current naughtiness level.

### An Overview

Section II opens with:

> Interactive fiction is a story in which *you* are the main character. Your own thinking and
> imagination determine the actions of that character and guide the story from start to finish.

It explains that the game keeps score and assigns a rank, and that "nothing happens until you type a
sentence and press the RETURN (or ENTER) key", so a player can plan a turn as slowly as they like:

> You should think of a locked door or a ferocious beast not as a permanent obstacle, but merely as a
> puzzle to be tackled. Solving puzzles will frequently involve bringing a certain item with you, and
> then using it in the proper way.

### Starting and Stopping

Booting, saving, restoring and quitting:

> To get past the opening screen and into the story, just press your RETURN (or ENTER) key. You will
> then get a description of the opening location of the story, Joe's Bar. Then the prompt (>) will
> appear, indicating that *Leather Goddesses of Phobos* is waiting for your first input.

The first exercise it sets the reader is \> INVENTORY, and it is careful about disks and the
Reference Card:

> To save your place in the story, type SAVE at the prompt (>), and then press the RETURN (or ENTER)
> key. Then follow the instructions for saving and restoring on your Reference Card.
>
> Remember when you RESTART or QUIT: ... you must first use the SAVE command.

**BRIEF**, **DIAGNOSE** (which gives "a medical report of your physical condition"), **INVENTORY**,
**LEWD**, **LOOK**, **OOPS**, **QUIT**, **RESTART**, **RESTORE**, **SAVE**, **SCRIPT**,
**SUGGESTIVE**, **STATUS**, **SUPERBRIEF**, **TAME**, **UNSCRIPT**, **VERBOSE** and **VERSION**
(which shows "the release number and the serial number of your copy of the story", to be quoted when
reporting a bug). Three of them are the naughtiness switch, each described in one line and
cross-referenced back to page 13. **STATUS** is the one that reports the settings:

> **STATUS**—This will give you a bunch of information: your current mode of descriptiveness, the
> level of "adultness" you're playing at, your score, a ranking based on your score, and the number
> of turns you've taken.

**WAIT** is given a paragraph of comic padding, promising that a player may "compose a sonnet about
the mating habits of baboons, and return to the story to find that nothing has changed."

### Tips for Novices

Eleven numbered pointers, the first being to draw a map of each location and the directions
connecting it to its neighbours:

> 1. Draw a map. ... There are 10 possible directions (NORTH, SOUTH, EAST, WEST, NORTHEAST,
> NORTHWEST, SOUTHEAST, SOUTHWEST, UP, and DOWN) plus IN and OUT.

The rest: examine everything, take everything, save often, read carefully, try everything, remember
that this story has many routes through it and some puzzles have more than one solution while others
"don't need to be solved at all", play with someone else, and — for a stuck player — the admission
that the package has a commercial escape hatch: tip 9 offers "a hint booklet and a complete map using
the order form in your package". The eleventh tip is a worked example of the parser tolerating
phrasing, and it ends with the game's own advice on a command that will not work:

> \> GET IN BED
> \> GET ONTO THE BED
> \> LIE DOWN ON THE BED
>
> ... If *Leather Goddesses of Phobos* still doesn't understand your command, you are almost
> certainly trying something that is not important in continuing your adventure.

One example on this page is the manual's own shrug at the player: "GIVE THE BASKETBALL TO THE LION"
brings the answer that the lion "takes an experimental bite out of the basketball but spits it out.
It continues to gnaw on your leg."

### Common Complaints

A page of the game's stock refusals quoted verbatim, with the cause explained between them. In the
manual's own wording:

> I don't know the word "______".
>
> You used the word "______" in a way that I don't understand.
>
> There was no verb in that sentence! Unless you are answering a question, each sentence must have a
> verb (or one of the special commands).
>
> Come again? You pressed the RETURN (or ENTER) key without typing anything.
>
> You can't see any ______ here!
>
> You can't go that way. There is no passage or exit in the direction you want to move.
>
> That sentence isn't one I recognize.

The last of these is the one to remember when a player's own sentence fails: the manual's example of
a sentence it cannot parse is "TAKE ROPE WITH READ", and its example of correct syntax is REACH UNDER
THE GARMENT.

### We're Never Satisfied, and the technical and legal pages

The house request for mail is printed with the names of the two Leather Goddesses themselves as
recipients:

> Here at Infocom, we take great pride in the quality of our stories, spending months on the tiniest
> detail. Even after the disks are "out the door," we keep improving, honing, and perfecting.
>
> ... If you find a bug, or if you think a certain puzzle was too hard or too easy, or if you just
> want to tell us what a lascivious group of degenerate perverts we are, drop us a note! ... Write
> to: ... Attn: TRENT or TIFFANY

Technical support is at (617) 576-3190, for bugs and not for puzzles; a replacement disk within
ninety days is free and afterwards costs $5.00, and the manual asks the owner to return the
registration card "if you'd like to be on our mailing list and receive our newsletter". The copyright
page asserts that "This manual and all other documentation contained herein are copyrighted and all
rights reserved by Infocom, Inc."

### Quick Reference Guide

The manual's last page is one page of summary. Its first point sends the reader back to "the separate
Reference Card in your *Leather Goddesses of Phobos* package", and point 4 explains the status line —
"a special line" giving "the name of your current location, your score, and the number of turns you
have taken so far in the story". It then splits every possible command into four kinds: direction commands (NORTH, SOUTH, EAST, WEST,
NORTHEAST, NORTHWEST, SOUTHEAST, SOUTHWEST, UP, DOWN, IN, OUT), actions ("just type whatever you want
to do"), commands given to other characters (name, comma, then what you want to say — "FRED, TELL ME
ABOUT THE STORM"), and special commands.

## The photograph on the last page

The final item in the scan set is a full-page colour photograph with no printed text of its own: a
staged still life on a rock ledge against a starfield — a string of white fairy lights, a chrome
torch standing upright, a small framed sepia portrait of a bespectacled man in a suit, a small framed
photograph of a blonde woman, a magnifying glass, a crumpled cloth, and a scatter of matchbooks and
tickets. Rolled paper and bright streamers hang over a blue and orange nebula. An orange booklet with
a round emblem on its cover lies open on the cloth, but its cover text is too small in this scan to
read.

With no platform, format or price line anywhere in the set, the box text identifying the machine and
version is not recoverable from these images.
