*What came in the Beyond Zork box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The scans show the box front, a second box panel which is a photograph rather than a printed text
back, the booklet **The Lore and Legends of Quendor** complete from its red cover to the product
code printed on its last page, and a map headed **Southland of Quendor**. **The instruction manual
was not among the scans**, so nothing in this file is quoted from it — there is no contents page,
no preface and no command list from the manual here, and no hints section.

## The edition these scans show

* The front carries a printed sticker naming the machine: `IBM and 100% COMPATIBLES`, `256K, MS-DOS
  2.0 or higher`, `1 disk drive (color monitor [...])`. The last word is not legible in the scan, and
  no disk size (3.5" or 5.25") is stated anywhere in these pages.
* The publisher's wordmark **infocom** stands at the lower left of the front panel, and appears again
  on the white label of the black box photographed on the second panel.
* The credit line on the front is **BY BRIAN MORIARTY**.
* The front also carries the line **INTERACTIVE FICTION PLUS**, with a small mark after it.
* The last page of the booklet — a plain cream page facing the Coconut entry — is stamped in the
  lower corner with the code **G-I28-09**.
* No ISBN, no price and no copyright line or date is legible anywhere in these scans.

## The box front

The title is built rather than set: **BEYOND** in orange letters floats above **ZORK**, and the four
letters of ZORK are cut as blocks of stone, split and drifting apart with shafts of light between
them. The sky behind is dark and ruled with fine horizontal lines in purple and pink; below the
letters the ground is a lit slope, and the whole panel is bordered top and bottom by dark bands.

The printed words on the panel, in the order they appear:

> BEYOND
> ZORK
>
> infocom
>
> IBM and 100% COMPATIBLES
> 256K, MS-DOS 2.0 or higher
> 1 disk drive (color monitor [...])
>
> INTERACTIVE FICTION PLUS
> BY BRIAN MORIARTY

## The second panel: a photograph, not a blurb

The other side of the box is given over to a staged photograph, and the scan shows no printed
paragraph of sales copy at all — what stands in its place is the scene itself: a dark rocky
landscape under an orange sunset, a mountain ridge silhouetted across the middle distance, a
bat-winged shape flying past the sun at the right, and, filling the upper part of the frame, the
face of a computer monitor displaying the game.

### What the monitor is showing

The screen is photographed at an angle, so its left side is out of focus. Its top bar is headed:

> Killtop

and under that heading the game prints a column of named attributes, each with a coloured bar beside
it, then a short passage of room description:

> [...] the narrow street that runs alongside a quiet [...]
> [...] the hill.
> [...] me on a hill overlooking the sea, a line of small trees stand[...] the road

The attribute rows are printed in this order:

| Row printed on the screen | What is beside it |
| --- | --- |
| Endurance | a red bar |
| Strength | a red bar, the longest of the set |
| Dexterity | a red bar |
| Intelligence | a red bar |
| Compassion | a red bar |
| Luck | a red bar |
| Group Class | a red bar, visibly the shortest of the set |

The third label is the least certain word in the panel — it is printed small and photographed at an
angle, and reads most like **Dexterity**. The others are clear enough to transcribe as printed. The
prose lines beside the room name are fragments: the screen is out of focus at the left, so only the
middle of each line can be read, and the gaps are marked here rather than guessed at.

### What the photograph shows of the contents

* The booklet from the box, lying open on the ground: a **red cover lettered in gold**, the title
  running down it exactly as it does on the printed cover scanned separately.
* Beside it a second, larger item with an **illustrated cover** — a coloured landscape with a round
  title medallion, the kind of panel a book or a box front would carry.
* A **black box** standing upright on the rock, with a white label bearing the **infocom** wordmark
  and a round red-and-white emblem below it.
* A **coconut** — a large brown nut-shaped object — resting on the ground behind the black box, with
  something green lying beneath it.

So the photograph is a picture of what the box contained, staged on a rock: the booklet, a box, and a
coconut, with the game itself glowing behind them.

## The Lore and Legends of Quendor

A small book, and the piece of reference material a player would have had on the table beside the
keyboard. It is covered in a **red grained cloth** and blocked in gold: the title **The Lore and
Legends of Quendor** in heavy Gothic letters running down the front, with a panel of gold knotwork
above it and another below it.

Inside, every page is cream, its edges ornamented with gold vines, leaves and bunches of grapes, and
each entry is headed by an **engraving in a gilt frame** — the picture above, the entry's name in
large capitals and the text beneath it. Entries are set two to a spread, one on the left page and one
on the right, and the book runs from a short author's note through twenty-two entries to a final page
given to the Coconut of Quendor.

### The author's note

The first page of text, opposite the Grue, carries no heading — only the signed paragraph that
explains why the book exists. It is printed with a large illuminated initial letter W:

> Within these pages is recorded certain knowledge regarding the flora, fauna, and locales of the
> kingdom. Although this wisdom has well stood the test of time, I would not wish to see it lost
> forever in the uncertain mists of the future. Therefore, for the enrichment of our heirs and with
> homage to our ancestors, I have writ into permanence the lore and legends of Quendor.
>
> —MNG

### Grue

Pictured over a winged helmet, a shield and a skull, with bones scattered across the ground:

> The grue is a sinister, lurking presence in the dark places of the earth. Its favorite diet is
> adventurers, but its insatiable appetite is tempered by its fear of light. No grue has ever been
> seen by the light of day; few have survived its fearsome jaws to tell the tale.

### Discipline Crab

Pictured as a pale crab on sand, standing high on its legs with both pincers lifted:

> Discipline crabs are small, moral crustaceans found in cellars, fallout shelters and other
> subterranean lairs. These brooding curmudgeons are deeply offended by the slightest intrusion; if
> cornered, they employ their razor-edged pincers with righteous efficiency.

### Eldritch Vapor

Pictured as a walled burial ground in a fog, with upright stones and a hooded figure among long
streamers of mist:

> Eldritch vapors dwell in cemeteries, moors and other locales where fog will hide their
> evershifting forms. Gleeful and mischievous, they enjoy snatching away the possessions of those
> foolish enough to wander into their realm. Visitors without possessions are themselves snatched
> away.

### Dorn Beast

Pictured as a close-up of a shaggy face, crowded with eyes above a mouth of long pointed teeth:

> Smart adventurers run the other way when they hear "Hurumph," the battle cry of the deadly
> dornbeast. Its 69 sensitive eyes can paralyze an unwary explorer with a single glare. Captured
> victims are plastered with round, sticky secretions that never come off.

### Monkey Grinder

Pictured as a showman in a cap and vest, both hands raised, standing behind the counter of a little
booth with a curtained window:

> Avoid this nightmare at any cost! Spawn of a carnival necromancer, the monkey grinder can blast
> minds to jelly with its powerful Sense Organ. Deceptively eloquent in both manner and speech, these
> loathsome creatures actually possess little intelligence, and suffer an illiteracy rate of 103%.

### Implementor

Pictured as four hooded figures seated together over a table of food — a joint of meat, fruit, and
cups in their hands:

> The Implementors are a race of minor deities who dwell on the Ethereal Plane of Atrii. Their ample
> free time is spent on costly luncheons where gossip and sweet nectars flow freely. Implementors do
> not discourage rumors that the world was created by them as a plaything.

### Giant Corbie

Pictured as a huge black carrion bird standing on bare ground, beak open and wings half lifted:

> Corbies are carrion birds with sharp eyesight and sharper beaks. Their color vision is so well
> developed, they can spot a yellow grotch in a hayfield from 200 bloits away. Corbies prefer the
> taste of dead, rotting flesh, but have been known to feast on live, running adventurers.

### Christmas Tree Monster

Pictured as a stand of conifers in mist, each trunk wrapped in wavy pale bands like tinsel:

> Vast herds of these luminous vegetables roam freely amid the glacial valleys of the south.
> Residents fear the autumn migrations, in which the trees cheerfully trample everything in their
> path. Christmas tree monsters are repelled by caterpillars, but nobody can explain why.

### Minx

Pictured as a close-up of a large fluffy animal, round-eyed, with a short tail curled above its back:

> Irresistably cuddly, the minx shares all the most infuriating characteristics of kittens, koala
> bears and piglets. Minxes are highly prized for their ability to find and root out chocolate
> truffles from the ground, and will eagerly devour them if given the opportunity.

### Unicorn

Pictured as two horses' heads, one bare and one carrying a long straight horn, with a chain and a
small bell or tag below the halter:

> Most unicorns have fled to the Plane of TransInfinite Splendor, where they enjoy a carefree
> existence free from the cruelty of man. The unhappy few left behind are eagerly sought by zoos and
> private collectors. It is good luck to kiss a unicorn's horn; but woe to any fool who harms one.

### Froon

Pictured from above as a dense walled town of tiled and domed roofs, courtyards, and flowering trees
planted along the walls:

> Legends of this magical kingdom date back before the reign of Entharion the Wise (0-41 GUE). Said
> to lie somewhere beyond the clouds, Froon was the setting for a series of beloved children's books
> by L. Frank Fzort, and later became a successful movie musical starring Judy Garlic.

### Hungus

Pictured as a woolly, sheep-like animal with a broad blunt head, standing over a small curled young
one lying on the ground:

> Part sheep, part hippopotamus, the hungus builds its nest in jungle swamps and other hot, squishy
> places. Normally docile and eager to avoid conflict or activity of any kind, the hungus is fiercely
> clannish, and will instantly charge at anything that dares to threaten its kin.

### Spenseweed

Pictured as a clump of broad-leaved plants growing between cobblestones, with the rim of a wooden
cartwheel standing behind them:

> The healing virtues of this common roadside plant are well documented. Although it is safe to eat,
> spenseweed is most effective when applied directly to wounds as a salve. Avoid the cheap commercial
> preparations, which may contain artificial coloring and preservatives.

### Chocolate Truffle

Pictured as an old man in profile, long-haired, holding a small round object to his mouth, in a bare
open landscape:

> Chocolate truffles grow only between the roots of oak trees. Dark brown when fresh, they decompose
> rapidly once exposed to air. Truffles were a favorite of Lord Dimwit Flathead the Excessive
> (770-789 GUE), who ordered the excavation of entire forests to indulge his bottomless appetite.

### Moss of Mareilon

Pictured inside a stone tunnel or shaft lined with timbering, where a pale mass of the moss has
spread down the wall:

> First classified in 843 GUE by Thwack of Mareilon, this soft, pale fungus thrives in underground
> tunnels and public toilets. When squeezed, the moss releases an invisible cloud of spores which
> improves the dexterity of laboratory rat-ants. Its effect on other species is uncertain.

### Compass Rose

Pictured as a single open rose with a small puff-cheeked face blowing at it from the upper right:

> The stem of this rare annual always droops in the direction of the prevailing wind. Rumors that the
> compass rose can actually control the wind are hotly denied by the Guild of Meteorologists, who
> harvested the species to the brink of extinction in the Rose Riots of 811 GUE.

### Morgia Root

Pictured as a bearded man in a soft cap with a short twig held between his teeth, hills and a river
behind him:

> It is a rare enchanter who does not carry morgia root to gnaw on during a long journey. The
> mint-flavored juice improves stamina, slakes thirst and conceals bad breath. In domestic
> applications, morgia root is often baked into pies, and makes an excellent platypus stuffing.

### Cruel Puppet

Pictured as a carved wooden head with a long pointed nose, exposed teeth and a ruffled collar, caught
in the act of shouting:

> Few creatures are more despised than the cruel puppet. It attacks by twisting itself into
> unflattering caricatures of its opponent, accompanied by jeers, rude noises and shocking
> accusations. Staunch monarchs have been reduced to tears by these merciless shapeshifters.

### Dust Bunny

Pictured as a huddled mass of them, close together, ears and eyes just showing in the fluff:

> Dust bunnies burrow in obscure corners and under furniture, and defend their territory by
> multiplying. They can clog a passageway in seconds, filling the air with dark, suffocating
> particles. Static electricity and lemon-scented sprays are their only natural enemies.

### Pheebor

Pictured as the ruins of a stone castle above a river, with a bridge and broken walls behind:

> Ruins of this ancient city are still visible at the confluence of the rivers Phee and Bor. The
> reason for its downfall (circa 400 BE) is unclear, but minstrels sing of a feud between Pheebor and
> its sister city Borphee over the naming of what is now called the Borphee River.

### Red Herring

Pictured as a single fish leaping clear of the water, with a spray of small grains scattered above
its head:

> These common fish patrol the dark recesses of freshwater pools and streams. But a handful of
> granola brings them racing to the surface, a fact known by every rural schoolboy. The old adage
> about red herrings being "good brain food" has no scientific basis.

### Lucksucker

Pictured in a dark loft, a black cat-like animal watching a man in a cap who holds a dangling rope or
cord above a trapdoor:

> Lucksuckers feed on good fortune. Part physical entity, part mental phenomenon, the sucker's
> appearance is based on the laws of probability, and may change without warning. Good luck charms
> provide only a temporary shield against attack. The best strategy is to run!

### Ur-grue

Pictured as a hooded figure in a black swirl of cloak, with a ring of bare skulls gathered at its
feet:

> Ur-grues are thought to be the shades of fallen Implementors. Skilled in black sorcery, the ur-grue
> can envelop itself in a personal zone of darkness which neither lamp nor flame can penetrate.
> Sunlight is the only thing it fears. It is unwise even to speak of this utterly evil entity.

### The Coconut of Quendor

The last entry, alone on a page of its own, and the only picture in the book that faces a blank
sheet — the cream page opposite carries nothing but the printed code **G-I28-09**.

Pictured as a coconut wearing a jewelled crown, with rays of light bursting out behind it and a
ribbon banded around it lettered **COCONUT OF QUENDOR**:

> Though reluctant to dismiss the Coconut outright, most historians regard its historical existence
> as dubious at best. Orkan of Thriff has suggested that if all the "Shards of The One True Coconut"
> and "Vials of The Blessed Milk" were gathered in one place, they would form a stack nine bloits
> high.

## Southland of Quendor

The scans also include a map — a large sheet, engraved and hand-coloured — with **Southland of
Quendor** lettered in a white cartouche in the lower right corner. The sheet is framed by a printed
border of knotwork in brown and gold, and a fleur-de-lis compass with a large **N** stands just above
the cartouche.

What the sheet shows, running from the top:

* A **river** along the northern edge, with a road and a cluster of buildings on its far bank, and
  mountains rising at the north-west.
* A **sea** down the whole eastern side, with a sailing ship under way and small boats nearer the
  shore.
* A **headland** on the east carrying a tall round tower — a lighthouse — above a cliff, with a road
  and walls running down to the water.
* A **harbour town** on the coast below it, with a long **pier** standing out into the sea, and a
  second town of red-roofed houses further up the coast.
* A large **forest** in the north-west and a second wooded region in the south, with scattered
  villages of red-roofed houses between them.
* A **river crossing** in the south-west: a long timber bridge or trestle carried across the valley
  on tall trestle towers, and smaller bridges further down the water.
* A **castle** of several towers with red roofs, walled and moated, at the western edge below the
  mountains, and a fortified gate or dam structure at the top of the map.
* Mountains along the entire western side, hatched in grey and brown.

The locations on the sheet are marked with **single capital letters** printed on it: **A** with
**B** below it in the north, **E** with **F** in the east, and others besides that are too small in
this scan to read with any confidence. The letter A stands at the centre of a round **medallion** —
a miniature compass rose with a ring of radiating points — and a dozen or so such medallions are
printed on the sheet like seals on the landscape. A few faint runs of very small light-coloured
marks lie on the open fields above one of them, so faint in the scan that they cannot be read; if
they are place names, they are not legible here.

What the letters name is not written anywhere on the sheet — no place name stands beside any of
them, and no key explaining them is among these scans. The map gives a player the shape of the
Southland and the letters to match against names heard in the game; the matching is left to be done.

## What a player had on the table

A red book small enough to keep beside the keyboard, and a map with letters on it. The book is the
whole of the world's reference material: its twenty-two creatures, plants, cities and magical things,
each with one paragraph of fact and one engraving, and one page at the end for the Coconut of
Quendor itself. The map is the geography that book describes — rivers, coast, forest, ruins, tower —
and the fourteen letters on it are the places a player will be told about by name and have to find
again on the paper. Everything else the box would have supplied, including the manual with the
game's commands and its hints, is absent from these scans and is not described here.
