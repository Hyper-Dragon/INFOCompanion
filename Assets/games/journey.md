*What came in the Journey box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box held: the game disk; the **instruction manual** (the pages here run from the contents page
to page 10); a folded **map of the Coastal Plain**; and a physical prop — **a small clear crystal
in a red velvet drawstring pouch**. The box back was not among the scans.

Two things about this release are worth stating before anything else, because they decide how the
game is played.

**It is played by choosing, not by typing.** *Journey* is one of Infocom's menu-driven stories —
you are given the party's possible actions and you select one. There is no sentence parser to
compose, so a companion used to typing `take lamp` has to know that here the move is a
**selection**, made with a mouse, a joystick, or the keyboard's arrow keys and RETURN.

**You are the party, not one person.** The four characters are Bergon, Praxix, Esher and Tag, and
the story is told as Tag's diary — every decision is yours to make, for the group and for each
individual in it.

## The box, in its own words

**The front**: **JOURNEY** in tall red letters on a blue ground, *The Quest Begins* beneath it in
white script, over a painted scene — four travellers by torchlight against a dark hillside, an
older woman in a blue hood at the front with a burning brand, a bearded man with a sword beside
her, a young woman and a man behind. Corners: *"Created by **Marc Blank**"*, the **INFOCOM
HOLE-PLAY / CHRONICLES** mark, and the platform panel — **IBM PC XT/AT & Tandy 1000**, with GGA,
VGA, MCGA, Tandy and EGA modes, 3½" disk.

## The manual: the setup

**The Preface** is the state of the world and the errand, and it is the reason the party sets out:

> A shadow of evil has fallen over the land. The crops are blighted, the earth scorched from lack
> of rain. Disease and famine ravage the once-prosperous people, whose hope of salvation fades
> with each passing day. **The only hope left is to find the wizard Astrix**, in faint hope that he
> will emerge from his distant solitude and offer his assistance. **A party was dispatched, but
> they haven't been heard from since their departure months ago.** Now, with winter's hardships
> looming on the horizon, **a second expedition** has been assembled and will soon embark on a
> fateful *Journey* into the unknown.
>
> The party of four is led by **Bergon**, a strong and fair man and a carpenter by trade. The
> Wizard, **Praxix**, wise yet unpredictable, provides the party with knowledge of lore and magic.
> **Esher**, the well-respected (if somewhat curmudgeonly) physician, has also been asked to make
> the trek. Finally, **Tag**, an apprentice food merchant, has agreed to tend to the party's
> provisions and to make a diary of its adventures. **The tale is told by Tag and the story of your
> *Journey* is seen through his eyes.**
>
> Whether your party meets the fate of its predecessors or returns triumphant is in your hands. In
> *Journey*, the decisions you make for the party and its members are woven into an epic tale of
> danger and mystery, of breathtaking beauty and heartbreaking loss, of desperate struggle and,
> finally, of ultimate triumph… **unlike other games you may have played, there are virtually no
> dead ends. Any action you take will advance the story toward one of its many endings. But there
> is only one ending that is the best.** To lead the party to its ultimate victory is a challenge
> worthy of only the most brave and most clever adventurer.

So: **a search for one wizard, with a vanished previous expedition ahead of you, and a winter
coming.** The "no dead ends, many endings, one best ending" line is the manual's own statement of
how the game treats failure — worth remembering, because it means an ending that seems final
usually is not the end of the story.

## The screen, and how a move is made

The manual prints the layout, and knowing it matters because the game's whole interface is these
three panes:

* **A — the story.** The text appears here; *"frequently, however, there is more to be told than
  can fit within this area. In that case, the word 'MORE' will appear at the bottom… you can view
  the rest by pressing any key."*
* **B — the illustration.** *"You will glimpse the world of Journey through the handsome
  illustrations that appear here."*
* **C — the selections**, itself divided into three columns.

**The three columns of the selection area** are the actual interface:

1. **PARTY COMMANDS** — *"those actions which are taken by your entire party as a group. Most
   often, these will involve moving from one place to another."* In the example screen they read
   **Proceed** and **Enter**, with **Game** always as the last of them.
2. **THE PARTY** — the four names, and *"when your Journey begins, there are four: Bergon, Praxix,
   Esher, and Tag. During the course of your Journey, **characters may be added (by choice) and
   lost (rarely by choice)**; all such changes will be reflected here."*
3. **INDIVIDUAL COMMANDS** — which change with the circumstances: in the example, **Get Advice**
   and **Cast** for Bergon and Praxix, **Examine** for Esher, **Drop** and **Inventory** for Tag,
   *"options change frequently during play to reflect the circumstances of the story (e.g. Tag
   might have an option to Pick Up if there were anything on the ground to take)."*

**Selecting**, with each device: *"Mouse: Point at, then click on, your desired selection. Joystick:
Move the joystick to highlight your desired selection, then press the joystick button. Keyboard:
Use the arrow keys to highlight your desired selection, then press the RETURN or ENTER key."* Many
selections then ask for more information (*"e.g., which spell to cast, which object to take, which
legend to tell"*) — *"simply select your choice from the list, or select [cancel] to change your
mind"*. For keyboard players: **the space bar moves the highlight between the PARTY COMMANDS and
INDIVIDUAL COMMANDS sections**; typing a choice's first letter highlights *and* selects it (with
the highlight in the right section first); and **`[cancel]` is BACKSPACE, the back arrow, or
DELETE**.

**The initial menu** offers five things: **Start** (sets you off), **Background** (the events
leading up to your Journey — *"if you are playing for the first time, it would be worthwhile to
read this prologue before selecting Start"*), **Change Name** (rename the narrator from Tag to a
name of your own choosing), **Help**, and **Game**.

## Save, restore, and the function keys

**The Game option** *"is always available as the last of the PARTY COMMANDS, at the lower left of
the screen"*, and it opens a menu that has no effect on the story:

* **Save** — *"make a 'snapshot' of your position in the story (a bookmark, if you will)… Unlike
  other games you may have played, *Journey* tries to lessen the interruptions you suffer by
  needing to frequently save your game place. Indeed, you will probably enjoy the flow of the story
  more if you avoid using Save regularly during your first time through the story."*
* **Restore**, **End Session** (Save, Quit, or Restart from the beginning), and **Controls** —
  **Script On** to make a printer transcript (a menu asks whether to include the party's commands
  or not), **Script Off**, **Refresh** (*"in the unlikely event that it should become garbled by a
  power glitch or other such nuisance"*), **Version** (release and serial number — *"please include
  this information in any call to Customer Support"*), and **Check Disk**, which verifies the
  disk's contents.

And the function keys, since they shortcut all of it: **F1 Save, F2 Restore, F3 Restart, F4 Quit,
F5 Script On/Off, F6 Refresh, F7 Version** — with Restart and Quit needing the same key pressed a
second time to confirm.

## The hints, and the manual's tips

The hint system here is unusual and worth knowing about, because it is not available on demand:

> *Journey* will be long and challenging, and it is unlikely that you will see it through to a happy
> conclusion until you have played for some time. Do not fear — all of the obstacles and pitfalls
> you face can be overcome in a logical manner; yet some may initially baffle even the most
> sophisticated player.
>
> **If you should reach an unsatisfactory ending, you will have the option of reading some of the
> narrator's "musings"**, thoughts made in hindsight about the decisions made during the *Journey*.
> Each "musing" refers to a specific point in the story where you may need some additional guidance.
> Of course, rather than using these musings right away, it might be more enjoyable to first
> experiment with different courses of action.

The **Tips for enjoying Journey** page says the rest:

* *"Try to play as much as possible without overusing Save — remember, all puzzles can be solved
  logically and without resorting to 'try-and-die, brute force' methods."*
* **"You might find it appropriate to make a Save each time you visit the Wizard Astrix."** — the
  one place the manual recommends saving, which says something about the shape of the story.
* *"There are no 'dead ends' in Journey; feel free to experiment and take chances. Every action you
  take will cause the story to move forward."*
* *"Utilize the options available to you judiciously; if more than one character can take the same
  action (e.g. examining something) do not assume that the result will always be the same."*
* And the keyboard shortcuts again.

## The map of the Coastal Plain

A painted map, and the game's world: **the Coastal Plain** sweeps down the left-hand side to the
sea. On the coast, from north to south, stand **Lavos** (a small hamlet, inland) and **Lendros**
(a larger town at the coast's edge). Inland is the **Narwish Realm**, a broad land of low green
hills and small lakes with a volcano marked **Sunset Mountain**; east of that is **Tanglewood**, a
dark green forest with a walled settlement and bridges drawn inside it, threaded by **the Black
River**, which runs down from the mountains in the north-east, over falls, and away east. Beyond
the forest rise the northern mountains — two peaks smoking, one of them drawn in eruption — and,
further south-east, **Sunrise Mountain**. At the map's bottom-right, on the coast, sits **Zan**, a
castle or walled town at the edge of the water. The sea is **the Great Waters**, with the **Strait
of Sézan** running between the plain and three islands: **Estril Isle** and **Misty Isle** to the
south, and **Lethor Isle** to the east; a great green sea-serpent is drawn in the western water, a
spouting whale further out. There is a compass rose at the top-left, pointing north.

A **dashed line** wanders across the whole map — out of Lendros, east across the Narwish Realm,
past Sunset Mountain, into Tanglewood, and on towards the eastern edge. No key explains it, but it
is the only route marked on the sheet, and the manual's party commands are *Proceed* and *Enter*.

## About the making of it

The manual's last pages are the credits, and they are useful mainly for knowing what kind of
author this is:

* **About the author** — *"Marc Blank, a graduate of MIT and the Albert Einstein College of
  Medicine, is one of the founders of Infocom. He co-authored the original mainframe version of
  *Zork* in 1977 at MIT, and went on to become one of the pioneers in the field of interactive
  fiction. At Infocom, he co-authored the *Zork* Trilogy and *Enchanter*, and was the sole author
  of *Deadline*, the first interactive mystery, and *Border Zone*, Infocom's first tale of
  intrigue."* He "considers *Journey* to be his second greatest departure from traditional
  interactive fiction, the first being his move to Southern California in 1986."
* **About the artist** — *"Donald Langosy is a Boston artist whose drawings and oil paintings can
  be found in private and museum collections around the world. He has recently discovered a new
  medium in the computer, creating unique illustrations for Shōgun and Journey."*
* **If you have technical problems** — write to Infocom, 125 CambridgePark Drive, Cambridge, MA
  02140, *"Attn: Dread Lord"*; the support line (617) 576-3190 takes bugs and technical problems
  but **not** hints; a disk failing within ninety days is replaced free; and returning the
  registration card registers you as *"the proud owner of an Infocom Role-Play Chronicles story"*
  and brings a subscription to *ZQ*.

## The crystal

The prop is a single clear quartz crystal, about the length of a finger joint, in a drawstring
pouch of dark red velvet. The manual pages in the scans do not mention it, so nothing here claims
what it does; it is recorded because it came in the box, and because a person who still has the box
has one, and will remember it.
