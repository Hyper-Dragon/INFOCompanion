*What came in the Wishbringer box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box held: the game disk; a folded map headed **POSTAL ZONES — FESTERON, ANTHARIA &
Vicinity**; a thin booklet from the Festeron Free Public Library, **Local History Series No. 4**;
the due-date card from a Festeron Town Library book; and a letter, written in a blotted and
hurried hand, which arrived in a Special Delivery envelope addressed to the Proprietor of Ye Olde
Magick Shoppe.

## The letter, word for word

> DELIVER THE MAGICK STONE TO ME BEFORE THE MOON SETS OR YOU WILL NEVER SEE YOUR CAT AGAIN!
>
> — The Evil One

## The envelope it came in

`THE TOWER` is written in the top left corner, in the same heavy hand. There is a SPECIAL DELIVERY
stamp, and a purple 90.8 stamp showing a platypus, captioned `EPLATYPUS UNUM`. The address:

> Proprietor
> Ye Olde Magick Shoppe
> North Festeron, Antharia
> 23-52-1

The postal code is the tell. Per the map's own legend, **23-52-1** is not a street address: it is
the code for the **Outlying Regions** — any place outside the Primary Postal Grid. So the letter
came from somewhere off the edge of the map, and the sender meant nobody to walk there by
accident.

## The map

Entitled **"Postal Zones — Festeron, Antharia & Vicinity"**, ruled into grid squares A–E by 1–5,
with north at the bottom-right compass. The **Primary Postal Grid** is the boxed middle of the
map. Everything outside it is "classified Outlying Regions and are designated by code no.
23-52-1"; the sheet is marked *"For use by registered Antharia postal service personnel only"*.
Drawn on it:

- a large house on a hill at the top of the map, across a water channel, reached by a long
  winding road down the cliff — Hilltop, where the Post Office is;
- the village below: the church, cottages, and the fountain in the open square at its centre;
- the **Arcade**, on the east side, standing out over the water;
- the **Library**, south of the village;
- the walled **cemetery** in the south-west, with its gate, trees and tombs;
- a cottage at the bottom of the map, marked, in a round hand, **"poodle"**.

## The Local History Series booklet, No. 4

Its title page describes it as *"A Moral History, in Verse, of the MAGICK DREAM-STONE, recently
unearthed by the Society of Thaumaturgic Archaeology... including a substantial body of hitherto
unpublished discoveries regarding the mystical properties of said Artifact."* Reproduced by the
Historical Museum of the Festeron Free Public Library; Violet Voss, Curator. Five chapters and an
epilogue:

1. **Of Morning-Star's birth and great beauty, and how she was abducted by the envious Queen
   Alexis.** In the reign of Anatinus, King of Misty Island, a peasant daughter was born of rare
   and perfect beauty — Morning-Star. Beside the throne sat Queen Alexis, heavy-hearted: her own
   newborn daughter, cursed by fate and prophecy, was sightless. How the baby Morning-Star, more
   beautiful and perfect, made her jealous! Envy breedeth Evil: the Queen caused the simple
   peasant home to burn; the sleeping family perished — all but Morning-Star, who, being rescued
   by the Queen's design, became her daughter, sight restored by Prayer. (The one true Princess,
   left behind to fill the vacant cradle, perished too, and never saw her mother.)
2. **Of Morning-Star's coming of age, and of the many knights who sought her fair hand in
   Marriage.** The years were kind; her beauty blossomed like the water-lily into full maidenhood;
   there befell her seventeenth birthday. Anatinus made it known that whosoever desired her hand
   should come forth, and by the custom of the kingdom the groom must first fulfil a Love-Quest of
   the Queen's own choosing. Many knights came. Alexis, dark with envy, watched the lusty swains
   descend like vultures and vowed in secret not to let them have her. Six were chosen, and stood
   before her for testing.
3. **Of the impossible Love-Quests devised by the crafty Queen Alexis, and how the six knights
   fared by them.** One, a lad but one-and-twenty, was sent across the sea to beg Lord Nimbus,
   God of Rain, to quench the thirsting fields; Nimbus was not sympathetic, and smote his vessel
   with a bolt of lightning. The second, a weapons-bearer strong of limb and spirit, scaled the
   mountain peak of Matter-Horn to seek Advice from spirits; the hopes of Princess Morning-Star
   fell with him. A third knight tried the fabled **Wings of Icarus**, to learn their secret
   method of Flight, and flew into the open maw of **Thermofax, a Dragon**. A fourth was sent
   deep into the **Mines of Mendon** to slay a **Grue** and drag the carcass up where all might
   see it, but Darkness overcame him; lost without a lamp, he was soon Devoured. The fifth was
   sent to steal the **Cocoa-Nut of Quendor** and chanced upon a lair of hungry **Implementors**,
   and did not Foresee his peril. Lastly stood before the Queen a gentle boy, no older than the
   Princess, whose beardless smile Morning-Star liked well, and she begged her mother not to test
   his Luck too harshly. But Alexis caused the youth to spend an evening amidst an unclean
   Cemetery, from whence he ne'er returned; for eldritch Vapors carried him away, and gave no
   reason.
4. **Of the Edict of Alexis, the demise of Morning-Star, and the discovery, many years after,
   of a Magick Stone, called Wishbringer.** Alexis cried *"Is no man in the kingdom fit to wed my
   only daughter? Methinks she must remain unmarried, then."* Morning-Star hoped death might grant
   her Freedom by her mother's timely passing; but the Reaper, busy elsewhere with a Plague,
   heard not her praying; so Alexis lived, and laughed, and watched her daughter's beauty fade
   away, and all her Wishes dwindle in her bosom. Many kingdoms after, when the reign of Anatinus
   was forgotten, a Scholar came to the Misty Isle and, amid the crumbling tombs of monarchs,
   chanced upon the mortal relic of the Princess. All was Dust, except her Heart, which — hard and
   shrunken to a pebble in the grave — was shining brightly with the stifled Wishes of her
   lifetime. Thus the Magick Stone of Dreams was discovered.
5. **Of the Seven Wishes, and what ye must know to invoke them.** Seven even is the number of the
   Wishes bound into the Stone; and if ye speak a Wish, that wish is Spent, and lost forever. Also
   know that ye must hold the Wishing Stone within thy hands to wield its Magick. The booklet then
   lists the Seven Wishes and the means of invoking each:

| the Wish | what it does, and what it needs |
| --- | --- |
| **RAIN** | falls only for the bearer of the Stone who standeth under an **Umbrella** |
| **ADVICE** | may bring wise counsel to the bearer who listeneth to **Sea-Shells** |
| **FLIGHT** | shall bear the Magick-wielder swiftly home, if ye be sitting on a **Broom-Stick** |
| **DARKNESS** | blacker than the Night, shall fall across the land if **Milk of Grue** thou drinkest |
| **FORESIGHT** | lifts the veil of Time, and shows the Future — but prepare thy eyes with **Glasses** |
| **LUCK** | will bring good Fortune, if ye hold a **Horseshoe** and the Stone in thy possession |
| **FREEDOM** | springs the dreamer from confinement — but mark well that ye first hath eaten **Candy** |

There is an **EPILOGUE**, and that is the end of the book.

## The library card

A small ruled card from the **Festeron Town Library** — the sort kept in the pocket of a borrowed
book. Dates have been stamped down its columns over the years, and along the bottom it prints its
little plea: *"Please take care of me... library books belong to everyone."* Somebody's book never
went back.

## A note from the shelf (not from the box)

**Careful where you walk.** Wishbringer is not a story you can wander through to see what
happens. Some steps are fatal, and they are fatal immediately: no warning, no second chance, and
nothing to be done about it afterwards but start again. So have a reason before you take a step.
Knowing why you are going is not the same thing as being reckless. If you are going because you
have run out of ideas, stop and say so instead, and look again at what you already have. A turn
spent thinking is cheaper than a run that ends.

Everything else is yours to find out.
