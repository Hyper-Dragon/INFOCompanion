## The Flathead Calendar

### What the calendar is

A printed calendar of the Empire, addressed as much to a citizen of Flatheadia as to a player. Its
in-world publisher names itself on the title sheet: the **Flatheadia Chamber of Commerce** and the
**Frobozz Magic Calendar Company**. The year is printed throughout as **883**, and every date in it
is given in **GUE** years. The months are **Estuary**, **Frobuary**, **Arch**, **Oracle**, **Mage**,
**Jam**, **Jelly**, **Augur**, **Suspendur**, **Ottobur**, **Mumberbur** and **Dismembur** — twelve
invented month names, each sheet devoted to one of the Twelve Flatheads. The seven day names are
**Sand Day**, **Mud Day**, **Grues Day**, **Wands Day**, **Birthday**, **Frob Day** and **Star Day**,
and every sheet prints the same line at its foot:

> Frob Day is a trademark of the Frobozz Magic Day Company.

### The cover sheet

**The Flathead Calendar** is set in a fat script across the top, with a sprig of leaves above it.
Below is a band of twelve small engraved portraits — six across the top, six in a second row — with
the year **883**, large, in the gap at the centre of the second row. No publisher's line is printed
on the cover.

### The title sheet

A single page of prose, boxed in the middle of the sheet under the printed title **The Twelve
Flatheads**, and worth quoting in full because it is what tells a reader whose faces are on the twelve
sheets.

> *As every student of history knows, the Twelve Flatheads were the greater part of the Thirteen
> Significant Accomplishments of King Mumberthrax the Insignificant.\**
>
> *In the immortal words of Boswell Barwell, the royal biographer:*
>
> Mumberthrax's place in history was secured by the one thing at which the Flatheads tended to
> excel: procreation. He sired twelve amazing children; twelve offspring who would transform the
> kingdom. As these magnificent siblings grew in notoriety, as their vast achievements became
> legendary, they became known as The Twelve Flatheads.\*\*
>
> *In 783 GUE, the coronation of Lord Dimwit Flathead the Excessive, Mumberthrax's firstborn, began
> at Flatheadia. This calendar, brought to you under the auspices of the Flatheadia Chamber of
> Commerce and the Frobozz Magic Calendar Company, celebrates the centennial of that memorable
> occasion.*
>
> *Reproduced for this calendar are Leonardo Flathead's famous portraits of the Twelve Flatheads.\*\*\*
> Leonardo brilliantly captured the varied personalities of the siblings on canvas over a span of
> seventeen years, starting with his own self-portrait in 766 GUE (see Jelly) and finishing with his
> Coronation Portrait of King Dimwit in 783 GUE (see Estuary).*
>
> *We are grateful for permission to reprint the accompanying excerpts from Boswell Barwell's
> exhaustive biography, "The Lives of the Twelve Flatheads."\*\*\*\**

The four footnotes, printed small at the bottom of the sheet:

> \* The thirteenth accomplishment was a decree that made Double Fanucci the National Sport of
> Quendor. Legends say that Double Fanucci was invented by Zilbo III, the last king of the Entharion
> dynasty. Double Fanucci Championships had been an annual event since 691 GUE, and Mumberthrax's
> Proclamation of 757 simply gave the sport official royal approval.
>
> \*\* From the introduction to "The Lives of the Twelve Flatheads."
>
> \*\*\* The originals can be seen in the gallery at Flatheadia Castle. Acknowledgement is gratefully
> made to Winifred Booblort of the Flatheadia Castle Preservation Society for her invaluable help.
>
> \*\*\*\* Copied right in 804 GUE by the Frobozz Magic Biography Publishing Company.

So the calendar's own fiction is that every portrait on it is a Leonardo Flathead canvas, and that
each biography is an excerpt from Boswell Barwell's *The Lives of the Twelve Flatheads*. The dates to
hold on to are **783 GUE**, the centennial the calendar exists to mark, and **766 GUE**, Leonardo's
self-portrait.

### How the month sheets are laid out

Every month sheet is the same object: a large engraved portrait of one Flathead at the top, the
biography beside it in two columns of small type, and the month's grid filling the lower half under
the month name and year — **ESTUARY 883**. Each sheet prints a note inside the grid: a **KRAZY
KWOTES** quotation, a **TID BITZ**, **FUN FAX** or **DID U KNOW?** paragraph, or a **QUICKIE QUIZ**
whose answer is printed upside down beneath the question. Festivals, moons and anniversaries are
printed small inside the day's square.

One rule matters before the months are read: **"Dimwit's Birthday Observed" is printed in the
Birthday column of every row of every month**, so its day number moves with the month. The days are
listed under each month below. A note in Estuary explains what the holiday has become:

> DID U KNOW? Dimwit's Birthday, now associated with big sales at U-Mart and J.C. Zorkmids, was once
> a day when everyone in the kingdom was required to give the king a present.

## The blueprint

A single landscape plan sheet, drawn in plain black line on white, inside a double-ruled border. The
title block in the bottom right corner reads exactly:

> Frobozz Magic Construction Company | 1 of 1
>
> Rockville Estates
>
> Phase Two, showing all work
> completed through 29-Mum-880
>
> Scale 1:1440 | drawn by S. Fzortbar

A very small code printed at the right of the "completed through" line cannot be read at this scan
resolution and is not guessed at here. Note the date: **29-Mum-880** — "Mum" is the calendar's
Mumberbur, three years before the calendar.

### The plan itself

The drawing is a lattice of **sixty-four octagonal room symbols numbered 0 to 63**, in eight rows of
eight. Short straight lines join some octagons to their neighbours — horizontally, vertically and
diagonally — and where there is no line there is no passage. The links that can be read with
certainty at this scan size are:

* **5—6—7** across the top row
* **12—13**, **22—23**, **26—27**, **30—31**, **34—35**, **38—39** within their rows
* **42—43**, **44—45**, **46—47** in the same row as one another
* **56—57—58** and **61—62—63** along the bottom row
* a line running east from **47** to the label, in capitals, **To GUH-95**

The rest of the lattice cannot be followed reliably at this resolution: the shape of the estate is
legible, the individual connections between the middle octagons are not.

Under the lattice is what the builders have not yet done:

> Work still to be performed in Phase Two:
>
> * Removal of temporary passages
> * Installation of emergency exits
> * Installation of sprinkler system
> * Construction of Concierge apartment

The list is the blueprint's own housekeeping note, and it says plainly that the passages are
**temporary** ones and that a concierge was to live somewhere on the estate.

### The note written on the sheet

In the empty left-hand margin, written by hand at a steep angle in heavy capitals:

> GODBAR—
> I LEFT MY HARDHAT
> OUT IN LOT Q.
> PLEASE PICK IT UP
> THANKS,
> QUIZBO

The letter after **LOT** is a fast scrawl; it reads here as **Q**. The signature's last letter is
written with a flourish and may be **QUIZBOD**, but **QUIZBO** is what the strokes support.

## The parchment leaf

A single loose leaf of mottled pale parchment with a serrated, torn edge on every side, photographed
flat on white with no caption or title. Everything on it is hand-drawn and hand-written in violet-blue
ink, in a large loopy cursive running diagonally across the leaf. The leaf's left margin and right edge
fall outside the scan, so the beginnings and ends of several lines are missing; nothing has been
filled in to cover them.

### What is drawn on it

* **A bulb emblem**, upper left: a light bulb with a hatched screw base and two dark round marks inside
  the glass, ringed by short radiating strokes as if it were a sun.
* **A violent scribble**, centre right: a jagged zigzag like a lightning bolt or a slash, with short
  dashes flicked out around it.
* **A star diagram**, bottom centre: a five-pointed star drawn over a triangle, with arrows pointing
  away to the lower left, equals marks at two of its points, and short parallel double-strokes beside
  and below it.
* **A small ruled grid**, right of centre: three by three, hand-ruled and slightly rotated; the
  legible marks are an **O** in the upper middle, an **X** in the centre, an **X** in the middle left,
  an **X** in the middle right and an **O** in the lower middle, and the rest are too small to be
  certain of.
* **A boxed grid of numerals**, lower right, with the third column partly cut away by the leaf's edge:

> 6 | 1 | [3…]
> 0 | 6 | [0…]
> 3 | 1 | 6

  Below it, in the same hand, the word **flodc** — the last letter is ambiguous and may be an **e**.

* **Cut-off writing along the right edge**: letters descend the leaf's right side and are cut off
  square by the edge of the leaf, leaving single letters and fragments only.

### What is written on it

Two words across the top of the leaf, one at each corner, both in the same big hand:

> […]boz foozi!

at the upper left, the first word cut by the leaf's edge, and at the upper right:

> zipso furgalneti

Then the body of the leaf, six lines, each beginning outside the scan and each with its missing words
drawn as small star marks — transcribed here as `\*`:

> […]se cannot be halted
> […]pt by two items belonging
> […]of the \*\* accursed twelve!
> […]each \* \* the \*\* cauldron….
> […]sing it \* \* \* \* into \* \* \*
> […]when \* \* \* \* \* uttering the sacred word.

An honest reading of the leaf, then: two words in the corners, one already partly lost, and a
fragmentary rigmarole about two items, twelve accursed somethings, a cauldron and a sacred word, with
diagrams and two grids of numbers and marks. Which words matter, and what the numbers are for, is the
work the leaf was left in the box to make the player do.
