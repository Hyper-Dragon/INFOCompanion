*What came in the Witness box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box says its contents plainly: *"Hermetically sealed since 1938 inside every WITNESS package:
your WITNESS disk, a suicide note, telegram, matchbook, an issue of the Santa Ana Register and the
February 1938 Nat'l Detective Gazette."*

## What the box itself says the game is

*February 1938, Los Angeles.* FDR's New Deal is finally rolling. Hitler's rolling, too — this time
through Austria. But as **Chief Detective** for a quiet burgh on the outskirts of L.A., you've got
other fish to fry.

**One gilt-edged society dame is dead.** And now it looks like some two-bit grifter is putting the
screws to her multi-millionaire old man. Then you step in, and the shakedown turns ugly. You're
left with a **stiff and a race against the clock** to nail your suspect — unless you get nailed
first.

*"Nobody said a sordid family affair like this was going to be a cinch. Everyone from the knockout
heiress to the poker-faced butler may end up in the slammer before it's over. Ahead of you is a
Gordian knot of motives and alibis. And the only testimony you can trust is that of your own eyes —
because you are The WITNESS."*

## The suicide note

One sheet of monogrammed writing paper — *Virginia Clayton Linder* printed across the top — in a
shaken sloping hand:

> Monica Dearest—
>
> I can live with this sadness no longer. For twenty-nine years, your father has lived his own
> life without me. Now I am taking the only way out.
>
> Monica, you must blame yourself in any way for what I am about to do. Nor should you blame
> Ralph. The affair with him was only a futile attempt to prove I was a woman, not just a piece in
> Freeman's collection.
>
> Tell your illustrious father how deeply I regret soiling one of his precious 'collectibles'.
>
> Mother

## The Santa Ana Register

A full front page of **The Register — Santa Ana, California, Tuesday, February 1, 1938** (Vol.
XXXII, No. 54; three cents a copy, seventy-five cents a month). The columns you can read:

- **"BORAH WARNS OF WAR DANGER."** Senator William E. Borah (R., Idaho) interrupts the Senate's
  foreign-policy debate to warn the administration against repeating "the very things that brought
  on the World War"; the world "has gone mad" because other nations believe Britain and the United
  States are building up their navies in concert. Admirals and senators join the debate.
- **The L.A. business ball goes on without its guest of honour.** A photograph of the benefit
  dinner for the Goodwill Ambassador of the Year shows the toast being raised to **Guest of Honor
  Linder**, who could not attend: *"He was unable to attend due to the tragic shooting death of his
  wife on Sunday (see related story, Page 2, Column 8)."* The piece describes **Freeman Linder** —
  marine in China, founder of Pacific Trade Associates, devoted to Asian business and charities
  since 1900, the tenth recipient of the award — and his long-time associate and travelling
  companion **Yukio Matsuyama**, who stood in for him and spoke of Linder's life-long love affair
  with the peoples of the Orient.
- **The Wright shooting trial.** Nine men and three women in the jury box as a defendant describes
  going into a "white flame of rage" and shooting his wife Evelyn, 28, and his best friend John
  Kimmel, 35, in a compromising situation; defence attorney Jerry Giesler; the defendant a gassed
  artillery sergeant of the World War, "very nervous, very emotional" since his return from
  France; he pleads not guilty and not guilty by reason of insanity.
- **A missing boy.** Four-year-old Teddy Thompson, lost three days in the Mendocino mountains;
  Deputy Sheriff C. H. Lovell says the child's tracks stop where a mountain lion's begin.
- The cold wave below zero in the north-central states; the Hope Diamond photograph with Evalyn
  McLean and her daughter; a $6,000 verdict against the county over an ambulance crash; a San
  Diego church raised from a $16,000 mortgage at a Victory dinner; and the Alaska delegate's
  warning of "armed conflict in the Bering Sea" over Japanese salmon-fishing vessels.

## The National Detective Gazette

**Vol. 7, No. 2 — February 1938.** A fat pulp magazine of the trade, and inside it:

- **Dr. Raymond Klotz's column on "Investigative Machines of the Future"** — his *Pathos Parabola
  Hypothesis*. One day, he writes, machines with brains — "not flaccid gray cerebellums, but brains
  of humming wires, trembling electrodes and glowing cathodes" — will be doing the exhaustive
  legwork of ten, even fifty hawkshaws. All the information in the FBI's files will be
  electro-mechanically sealed inside gigantic **Electro Magic Brains (EMB)**. At the issue of a
  single cryptic voice command — his example: **"OKLIT VOS FROB VEN-VEN DOOBELDEE"** — the brain
  will regurgitate reams of information stored within its vast memory. Smaller versions will be
  linked to the main brain through an extensive wire system called a "meshwork", communicating in a
  special brain language known to only a select few law-enforcement officials; information will be
  stored permanently on tiny ticker-tape machines in a binary code of dots and dashes; packages of
  information and commands will be bundled into crime-solving "programs". Klotz claims ten years of
  service to the FBI, five of them, he says, with a dossier on every man, woman and child in the
  United States. The editor notes that as the issue goes to press Dr. Klotz has exiled himself to
  Walla Walla, Washington, to continue his EMB research, and that the *Gazette* prints even the
  most phantasmagorical of his postulates — "in the Nat'l Detective Gazette tradition." (The same
  page notes the *Gazette* has just acquired two mechanical adding machines for its accounting
  department.)
- **"TIPS FOR GREENHORNS" by Capt. Jock Barnes** — a retired LAPD veteran who walked the Angel
  City beat for over forty years (and now lives in Redondo Beach with his wife June and his dog
  Fang). His lesson is the death of his old partner **Paul Kelly**, killed in a domestic call they
  were too impatient to case first: *"Always case a joint before you start busting down doors. See
  who's who and what's what. Play it cool; don't be a jerk... You're a referee, not a
  participant."*
- **The five best bars in L.A.**, as chosen by a hundred-odd off-duty lawmen the magazine surveyed:
  1. **The Condor's Nest**, 2424 Caristas Springs Blvd., L.A. — dark and secluded, "everything but
  a snooker table". 2. **The Shasta Lounge**, Beacon Court, Hollywood — the best selection of
  single malts and imported ales. 3. **Fish Camp**, MacArthur Wharf, Long Beach — longshoremen and
  the law mix in this unpretentious warehouse bar. 4. **The Bel Pre**, 4162 Gardena Rd., Torrance —
  L.A.'s darkest and most secret rendezvous. 5. **The Brass Lantern**, corner Berez and LaVezza,
  San Fernandito — *"Where all good cops go when they need to get out of town. Try the Moo Goo Gai
  Pan!"*
- **Advertising** fills the back page: Peters Rustless ammunition, "The Ammunition That Inspires
  Confidence"; Peerless Handcuffs of Springfield, Mass.; and the usual persuasions of the trade.

## The matchbook

A red matchbook from **The Brass Lantern** — the fifth name on the *Gazette*'s list of the five
best bars in the city. On its cover: a round emblem ringed with the words **LONG LIFE** and **GOOD
LUCK**, the line **"Call ADeline 1308"**, the name in brush strokes — **THE BRASS LANTERN**,
*"Corner Berez & La Vezza"*, **San Fernandito** — and, along the lower edge, *"CLOSE COVER BEFORE
STRIKING."* Inside the cover, in blue ink, somebody has written a second number: **CHANDLER 1729**.

## How these papers are meant to be used

You are the Chief Detective: the telegram is a summons to you personally, and the note is the kind
of private paper that would be laid on a detective's desk. The Register and the Gazette are what
the city and the trade are reading that February; the matchbook is the sort of thing that falls
out of a dead man's coat, or waits in a detective's own pocket. Every fact in them is a fact of
the case as it stood in 1938 — which of them matter, and what they add up to, is for you to find
out.
