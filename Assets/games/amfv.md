*What came in the A Mind Forever Voyaging box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box held: the game disk; the instruction manual (not among these scans); **a map of downtown
Rockvil**; an issue of the in-world magazine ***Dakota***; a yellow ballpoint pen printed with a
2031 advertisement; and the game's copy-protection device, described in its own section below.

## The code wheel, and why it is not reproduced here

The package contained a **PRISM Project Facility "Class One Security Mode Access Decoder"** — a
two-part printed wheel, item number **603475**, valid **March 15–31, 2031**, carrying the warning
*"FOR AUTHORIZED USE ONLY. Protect PRISM project security. Do not leave this decoder in an
unsecured area."* It was the game's access code: the player turned the wheel, read a number off it,
and typed that number when the story (playing the part of the security system) asked for it.

**The scans in this collection include a photograph of the wheel, but what is printed on its two
parts is not transcribed into this file.** The wheel is a physical lookup device, and copying its
readings out would turn a description of the packaging into a working key — the one thing a sidecar
must not be. What is recorded here is what was in the box: a decoder, with those words on it, valid
for those sixteen days of March 2031.

## The box, in its own words

**The front** is a warm ochre field with the title **A MIND FOREVER VOYAGING** across the top in
white. At the centre is the face of a young man, looking up and away, and where his neck and collar
should be the drawing becomes **printed circuits** — fine traces and solder points running down out
of his jaw into his jacket. Around that portrait, in six panels, are the images of the world the game
takes place in: the American flag next to a monitor and a rat; a **VOTE** broadcast with cameras and
an antenna; a church full of people under an orange sky; a heavy boot with a globe and a torch; a
bandaged head beside a rat in a circle; and a bare room. Nothing on the front explains any of it,
which is the design of the thing — the panels are the game's America, and the face is the machine
that is going to walk through it.

## The ballpoint pen

A yellow ballpoint with a black clip, printed along the barrel —

> **QUAD MUTUAL INSURANCE**
> *"From seafloats to spacelfoats,*
> *you're covered by QUAD"*
> **Compucode 8-2268-05-376**

a bit of promotional swag from an insurance company that exists only inside the game's 2031. It came
in the box with the disk.

## The map: Downtown Rockvil, "Jewel of the Quad States"

A folding street map of the city the story starts in, printed in the four-colour style of a chamber
of commerce freebie: beige blocks, green parks, blue water, red arterials, and a compass rose. The
title block sits in the upper right —

> **DOWNTOWN ROCKVIL**
> *"Jewel of the Quad States"*
> SCALE: 1" = 1/4 MILE
> *This map provided courtesy of the*
> **ROCKVIL CHAMBER OF COMMERCE**
> **SKYCAR LEASING COMPANY**
> Printed in USNA, 11/2030

The **date** is worth noticing: the map was printed two months before the story's date, which is the
sort of detail a person holding it would enjoy.

**What the map shows.** The **Skycar Intercontinental Intercity Thruway** runs the whole length of the
western edge, with **Skyway 61** crossing the city north of centre. From north to south the sheet
carries the **Rockvil Reservoir** and the **Rockvil International Airport**; **General Craic J.
Manville Army Base**; the **Rockvil Botanical Garden**, the **Aquarium** on **Aquarium Drive**, and
the **Rockvil School**; **Bodanski Blvd.** and **Riverside Park** down the west side; **Halley Park**,
**Halley Drive** and the **Halley Museum** in the middle of the city; **Huang Hall**, **City Hall**,
**Rockvil University** on **University Avenue**, the **Main Library** on **Main Street**, **Parks
Street**, **Kennedy Park**, **Elm Street**, **Marlborough Street**, the **Infotech Building**,
**Midland Avenue**, the **North Central Station** with the **Railroad Museum** beside it, and
**Midland Cemetery**; then, along the river, **Church Street** and **Church Street Park**, the city
**Jail**, **River Street**, the **Riverwalk**, **Industrial Park Drive**, the **South Bridge** and the
**Rockvil Bridge**. The dense street grid between them is drawn street by street but unlabelled.

**The panel down the right-hand side** is the map's other half — *"DON'T MISS THESE EXCITING
ATTRACTIONS IN ROCKVIL, Jewel of the Quad States"* — and it is a small guide to the city, one line
each:

* **ACME COMPUTER CORP** — *"Visit this genuine 20th century computer factory, still a major employer
  in the Rockvil area."*
* **AQUARIUM** — *"Hundreds of marine creatures cavort in the large central tank of this popular
  attraction."*
* **HALLEY MUSEUM** — *"Halley Museum's outstanding collection of classical art is complemented by
  exhibits on modern art, science and engineering, natural history, and human history."*
* **HUANG HALL** — *"Huang Hall's ultra-modern auditorium, the largest in the quad state area,
  provides an ideal setting for cultural and artistic events as well as conventions and trade
  shows."*
* **INFOTECH BUILDING** — *"Marvel at this 130-story office tower, the tallest building in Rockvil
  and the sixth tallest in the world."*
* **NORTH CENTRAL STATION** — *"This historic national landmark provides a glimpse of the past for
  over a million yearly visitors, who browse the souvenir shops, ride the authentic electric train
  and view the extensive exhibits at the Railroad Museum."*
* **ST. MICHAEL'S CHURCH** — *"Lovely stained glass windows and marble statuary add to the grandeur
  of this century-old place of worship."*
* **ZOOLOGICAL GARDEN** — *"Spend a peaceful afternoon wandering amongst the herbal displays and
  natural animal habitats of the zoo."*

**In practice the map is the game's street directory.** Rockvil is where the story opens and where a
great deal of it happens, so this sheet is not illustration: it is the list of places a player could
type. The attractions panel in particular names buildings the story uses, and the *"four colo\[u\]r"*
convention — parks green, water blue, arterials red — is the player's only guide to which streets
run through to which.

## *Dakota* magazine

The feelie, and the piece of packaging that sets the game's tone: a full issue of an American
regional magazine — ***Dakota***, *"South Dakota's largest circulation magazine"* — dated **March
2031**, the year the story is set, and printed as a real magazine rather than as a game manual:
glossy pages, a painted cover, letters to the editor, feature articles with bylines and page numbers,
a profile, an advice column, and full-page advertising.

The device is worth stating plainly, because it is the reason this file exists in the box at all:
**the manual explains the machine, and the magazine explains the country.** *A Mind Forever Voyaging*
is set in the United States of 2031 — the USNA — and its story is about what has happened to that
country, told month by month in a periodical. So the magazine is contemporary voice: an ordinary
issue of an ordinary publication, with its own editorial line, its own advertisers and its own
opinions about the government of the day.

**How to read it when it comes up.** The magazine's article list, editorials, letters and
advertisements are the world's own claims about itself — the things the papers are saying in 2031 —
and that is exactly the material the story tests. Nothing in it is a puzzle answer; all of it is
testimony. A companion who has read it will recognise a place, a company, a public figure or a
policy when the game mentions one, and will know which of the magazine's own statements are being
sold rather than reported.

**Two of its pages were read closely enough to rely on** and are reflected above: the cover and the
contents page, which is how this file can state the issue's month, its year and its circulation
claim. The rest of the issue's pages were photographed at an angle and are not transcribed here
word for word — the scansion is not good enough to quote from safely, and a quotation that is nearly
right is worse than none in a file whose whole purpose is to tell a companion what the player had in
their hands.
