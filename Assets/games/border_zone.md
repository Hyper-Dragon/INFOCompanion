*What came in the Border Zone box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The back of the box lists the contents in the game's own words: *"Smuggled into every Border Zone
package: your Border Zone disk; 'Lam Frobnia' tourist guide and phrasebook; a business card from
historic Ostnitz; a Frobnia National Railway matchbook; and a map of the border."*

## What the box itself says the game is

Where the Iron Curtain divides East and West, the frontier is a no man's land between freedom and
captivity, a place where moments lost or precautions not taken are paid for in men's lives. In
**Border Zone** you cross that barrier not once but three times, as **three different characters**
in a fast-paced story of international intrigue.

- You see the story from **three viewpoints**, stepping into the shoes of a **different major
  character in each of the three chapters**. The chapters are set in separate locations on or near
  the border, and each is a complete story in itself, with its own conclusion.
- The tension is heightened by **real time**, which ticks on regardless of your actions. As you
  race against the clock to complete your missions you will be caught up in an adventure more
  suspenseful than any spy thriller you have read.
- The story begins **on the train to Litzenburg**, a peaceful country just outside the Iron
  Curtain. In the border town of **Ostnitz**, Constitution Day festivities include a speech by the
  American ambassador — but plans are afoot to destabilize this neutral territory by
  **assassinating the diplomat**.
- Speeding towards the border through the Eastern bloc country of **Frobnia** are **an
  easy-going American businessman, an ambitious young American spy, and a ruthless KGB agent**.
  All three are soon to become entangled in the assassination plot, their lives intertwining as
  each carries out his perilous assignment.
- The game has **on-line hints** built in, for when you need them — but hints take you only so
  far. Even when you know exactly what to do, **discretion and timing are crucial** as you outwit
  the KGB, evade a snarling pack of search dogs, make a desperate assault on the border, and count
  down the moments to the assassination.
- Written by **Marc Blank** (author of *Zork* and *Deadline*). The box's parting advice: *"So
  steel your nerves, and don't blow your cover, friend. You've a long way to go before you come in
  from the cold."*

## The tourist guide and phrasebook

A propaganda-bright booklet: **"FROBNIZ IZIM!"** across the top, three photographs (a farm truck
in a field; a woman in a headscarf; a woman in a good coat, smiling), then beneath them **"I AM
FROBNIA"**, the round badge of the **Frobnia National Tourist Council**, and the title: **"A
Fortunate Tourist's Guide and Phrasebook."** It is a guide to Frobnia and an English-to-Frobnic
phrasebook in one — the cover alone offers a working pair: **FROBNIZ IZIM** prints directly above
**I AM FROBNIA**.

**The back cover is a railway timetable:** *"TRAINS FROM FRZI TO VIENNA DAILY!"* — a grid of daily
services (Monday through Sunday columns, express and local) between **Frzi** and **Vienna**, with
morning, midday, afternoon and evening departures and arrivals late the same day or after
midnight, following routes **via Knukla**, **via Ostnitz**, **via Yinsli**, and **via Ostnitz &
Knuckla**, including a "weekend" express marked FSSu. Under it, the operator's line: *"Please see
agent for current schedule."* The footer: **FROBNIA NATIONAL RAILWAY — "You Will Go With Us."**

## The business card from Ostnitz

> RINK'S
> **Rare Books and Curios**
> *In Historic Ostnitz for 35 Years*
>
> Velkom! Pimsna! | Welcome! — Willkommen!

The greeting line runs in four languages at once: Frobnic and the local speech first, then English
and German. The same four-cornered greeting turns up on shop signs and tongues on both sides of
the frontier, which is what makes the border town what it is.

## The matchbook

Orange and gold, with a small stylised locomotive in a roundel: a **Frobnia National Railway**
matchbook. The name is printed in Frobnic: **"FROBNIZ BOURASHNI BALLNI"** — the same words the
timetable prints in English as *Frobnia National Railway*.

## The map of the border

The box also carried a **map of the border** — the frontier with its river, crossings and towns.
It folds out to sit beside you while you play: watch it for where the checkpoints are, and for
where the border runs when the characters start to move.

## How these papers are meant to be used

Every piece of this is period material — a real tourist guide, a real timetable, a real business
card, the kind of thing an agent in a border town would carry. None of it is a walkthrough. The
phrasebook in your hands is the only dictionary there is; when a Frobnic word goes by, that is
where it came from.
