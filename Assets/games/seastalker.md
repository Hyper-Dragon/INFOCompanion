*What came in the Seastalker box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The back of the box photographs its own contents and names them: the disk, the Submarine Logbook,
four top secret Infocards, an Infocard decoder, a nautical chart of Frobton Bay and the Discovery
Squad badge. The instruction manual itself is not among the scans — what survives here is the
*Scimitar* Logbook's cover and pages 2 to 6, the two sides of a red reference card, the chart, the
decoder, eight Infocards and the badge.

## The box front

**SEASTALKER** in heavy yellow letters, with the smaller line beneath: **DIVE DEEP INTO DANGER.**

The picture is a view from inside a submarine. A brass-rimmed porthole with bolts and rivets all
around it fills the middle of the frame, and water is running off its rim in beads. Beyond the
glass, pressed up against it, is something enormous, green and purple and spotted, half tentacle
and half coral, with a single eye — red-pupilled, ridged, unblinking — on the right of the pane,
and a small curled tentacle tip at the lower middle. The hull's instruments crowd the edges of the

the foreground, below the viewport, two gloved hands work a bank of controls — the left holds a red
joystick button, the right is splayed open on the console among three red toggles, a deep-sea
gauge, a compass-like dial and two illuminated screens.

## The Infocard Decoder

A piece of blue plastic, a little narrower and a little shorter than the Infocards. Printed on it
in white:

> **INFOCARD DECODER**
>
> Insert Infocard Here.

Below the title is a horizontal slot cut through the card, with a small oval notch at the middle of
its upper edge; below that, the words *Insert Infocard Here* sit above a large rectangular window
glazed with translucent **red** plastic. The bottom edge of the card has a shallow tab cut out of
it, so it can be stood or slid. The red window is the whole point: the Infocards are printed in
reds, and the hidden clue text on them can only be read through the red filter, which drops the
background field away and leaves the clue sentences.

## The red reference card, both sides

A cream card printed entirely in red — the card that comes first out of the box, since it is the
one that tells you how to play, and since it is what both the Infocards and the manual keep calling
the *Reference Card*.

> **IMPORTANT: READ THIS CARD BEFORE YOU BEGIN.**
>
> The object of the game is to save the Aquadome from danger. You'll have to navigate your sub, the
> *Scimitar*, to the Aquadome, and then start solving the mystery with the help of the people that
> work there.
>
> Getting started
>
> 1. To start the game ("boot up"), see the Reference Card that's inside your SEASTALKER package.
> 2. When you see the prompt (>) on your screen, SEASTALKER is waiting for your command. There are
>    three kinds of commands that SEASTALKER understands:
>    A. Direction commands such as GO WEST
>    B. Commands to do things such as LOOK AT THE BOOK
>    C. Commands given to people such as MARV, GIVE ME THE KEY
> 3. To go places, north, east, south, etc., just type the direction you want to go: N, E, S, W, NE,
>    SE, SW, NW, UP, DOWN, IN, OUT. (Notice that you can type the eight compass directions with one
>    or two letters.) Or type GO TO (a person, place or thing).
> 4. To do things, look at things, or explore places, just type whatever it is you want to do. For
>    example: GO TO THE AIRLOCK; or OPEN THE HATCH; or EXAMINE THE BOOK are all commands that you
>    might use. Once you are familiar with simple commands, you'll want to try some more complex
>    ones. Some examples of these can be found in the "Communicating with SEASTALKER" section of
>    your Instruction Manual.
> 5. To give commands to people, just type their name, then a comma, then the command. For example:
>    BILL, GIVE ME THE TOOL, or TIP, TURN ON THE SONARSCOPE.
> 6. Important! After every command, you should hit the RETURN (or ENTER) key. This will make
>    SEASTALKER respond to your command.
> 7. The special line on your screen is the status line. It tells you three things: the name of the
>    room or area you are in, your score and the number of turns you have taken during the game.
> 8. You can pick up and carry some items in the game. For example, if you type TAKE THE TOOL, you
>    will be carrying the tool. You can type INVENTORY (or I) to find out what you are carrying.
> 9. If you have any trouble playing, just refer to the Instruction Manual for more detailed
>    instructions and sample commands.
> 10. When you want to stop playing, save your place for later, or start over, see the instructions
>     in the "Starting and Stopping" section of your Instruction Manual.
>
> Some clues to help you get started.
>
> 1. Answer the videophone
> 2. Turn on the microphone
> 3. Ask Bly about the problem

That short list of directions and simple verb commands is the whole command language a Seastalker
player needs — this is a Junior-level game, and the card is written for a nine-year-old reading it
over the keyboard.

## The Infocards

### How the cards are printed

Eight cream cards, each the same size as the decoder. Every card is headed in red capitals with its
own number and the words **Hidden Clues**:

> Infocard #1 — Hidden Clues

and so on, 1 through 8. Below the heading, the entire face of the card is filled edge to edge with
the word **SEASTALKER**, repeated over and over — *SEASTALKERSEASTALKERSEASTALKER* — row after row
down the card, with pale gaps between the rows.

Down the left-hand side, three small grey numerals are printed among the sea of words: **3.** about
a quarter of the way down the card, **2.** near the middle, **1.** about three-quarters of the way
down. Each numeral marks the start of three short lines hidden in the field: the clues themselves.

The clue text is not readable on the card as it comes out of the box. Seen through the red decoder
it is — the pages filed here as `infocard-N-decoded.jpg` are the same cards photographed through a
red filter, and they come out as a flat red sheet with the clue sentences just visible in a darker
red. Read that way, each card turns out to carry three numbered fragments rather than three
complete hints, with words missing from the middle of them. Within the fragments, the words that
belong in the gaps are not readable in these scans — the gaps carry only the pale repeating
SEASTALKER field, so whether those words are hidden in the pattern's own red or simply left blank
on the printed card cannot be told from the scans that were made.

**The clues themselves are not transcribed here, on purpose.** They are printed to be unreadable
without the decoder, and reading them out would turn a description of the packaging into a puzzle
sheet. What was in the box was eight cards and one decoder, and that is what this page records.

## The Discovery Squad badge

A round sticker, glossy, the same emblem that is printed on the Logbook's cover and visible in the
photograph on the box back: a black-and-white killer whale breaching over a blue disc, ringed with
red letters — **THE DISCOVERY SQUAD** over the top and **DIVE DEEP ★ DIVE FAR ★** around the
bottom, with red stars separating the words. It is the badge the White House letter is
congratulating you on being elected to wear.

## One thing the scans leave out

There is no scan of the game disk or its sleeve beyond the photograph on the box back, where the
disk sits in a white sleeve lettered **SEASTALKER**. And the manual's own pages — the ones with the
sample commands and the "Starting and Stopping" instructions the reference card sends you to — are
not in this folder either; the Logbook here stops at page 6.
