## Instructions for folding your own paper crane

A single sheet, headed **CRANE** in large capitals, with the character **鶴** beside the heading and
three characters printed vertically down the left margin \[apparently **米国製**\]. Its text, in the
voice of a children's craft book, runs:

> Start with a square piece of paper. Results are best if you use thin paper at least 9" square.
> Pre-fold paper as shown. These creases will be used as guidelines.

The first diagram is the square with its diagonal and centre creases, the corners and fold points
lettered **a**, **b**, **c**, **d**, **e**, **f**, **g** and **i**.

> 1. Fold c to a.
> 2. Fold d to b.
> 3. Fold d to a.
> 4. Reverse-fold i out, bringing d to a. Turn over. Reverse-fold b in, bringing all points
>    together. Fold f to g and fold b to i.
> 5. Turn open end down. Fold g and b in along dotted lines. Reopen. Lift c halfway up. Fold down
>    forming crease between j and k. Lift c up and over e, then down. Bring side flaps in by folding
>    down so that g meets b.
> 6. Turn over and repeat.
> 7. Fold l and m top layers in. (Same as first folds in step 5)
> 8. Turn over and repeat.
> 9. Hold body at dot. Reverse-fold in b point, at the same time folding out along dotted lines. Fold
>    to form tail. Repeat with d point to form neck.
> 10. Form head by folding outside in, then down.
>
> Complete by partially folding down wings. Slowly open back by pulling wings gently apart.

Step 5 is printed as three columns of instruction beside one diagram.

## What the player has in their hands

Laid out, the package gives the player four working objects and a picture of a fifth:

* the **disk** (seen only in the box's photograph), labelled **TRINITY** in white;
* the **Illustrated Story of the Atom Bomb** — a twelve-page comic history, in "Quest For Knowledge
  Series" livery, from Bohr in Washington in 1939 to a general promising the missiles will never have
  to be used;
* the **Trinity site plan**, sheet **1 of 3** of the Army's drawings of the site: the McDonald ranch
  house room by room above, the desert target rings and roads below, the ranch site with its windmill
  and reservoir inset;
* the **sundial**, a punch-out Renaissance-style instrument with its motto, its Dickinson verse and
  its assembly instructions printed on the sheet;
* the **paper crane instructions**, ten numbered folds from a square of paper, with the crane's own
  characters in the margin.

The comic and the manual's preface point at the same moment from opposite directions: the preface
carries an American tourist in London forward to "the world's first atomic explosion, code-named
Trinity", and the comic narrates that explosion from 1939 through the countdown and past it.
Everything else in the package is the historical record as printed — the plan's rings and roads, the
booklet's captions and its running joke about the doubters, the sundial's motto that time devours all
things. What any of it is for is not written on any of it.
