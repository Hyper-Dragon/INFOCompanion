*What came in the Suspended box, transcribed from the package itself.*

The box sells a five-hundred-year sleep: your body frozen at the nerve center of a planet, your
mind awake in a network of computers, your hands replaced by six robots. Inside were the box, the
classified **Briefing for the Contra Central Mentality** (cover, both inside covers, every printed
page), two letters from Contra Central, the **Contra Central Mentality Lottery Card**, the folded
**Underground Complex** schematic, and a sheet of die-cut playing pieces.

The instruction manual itself was not among the scans: the briefing's last page says "The Table of
Contents for the manual is on page 11," and no page of that manual appears here.

## What the box says the game is

The back of the box opens with the promise, and then the break in it:

> They said you would sleep for half a millennium—an unreasonable length of time, considering
> you'd […] in limited cryogenic suspension. Your body would rest frozen at the planet's nerve
> center, an underground complex twenty miles beneath the surface. Your brain, they told you,
> would be wired to a network of computers; your mind would continue to operate at a minimal
> level, overseeing maintenance of surface-side equilibrium. And you would not awake, so they
> promised, until your five hundred years had elapsed—barring, of course, the most dire
> emergency.
>
> Good morning.

Then the game itself:

> In SUSPENDED, you will strategically manipulate six robots. Each has a distinct perception of
> the world, and offers you specific abilities. For instance, one specializes in sight, a second
> in hearing, and a third in information from computer memory. […] through them, you will solve an
> intertwined myriad of realistic and original problems. Should you find yourself baffled by some
> of the more intricate puzzles, you may wish to consult the underground complex's built-in
> advisory peripheral for hints.
>
> The first time you play SUSPENDED will not be your last… And even if you succeed in mastering the
> first level of play, an advanced second level waits in the program to test your mettle again and yet
> again. In fact, the game is so designed that you can alter conditions at will before starting,
> effectively allowing you to customize the game into a new kind of SUSPENDED whenever you desire.

Under **The Infocom difference—it's all in your head** the back cover pitches at a buyer who has seen
other adventure games, claiming for every Infocom game free-form English input, "the most complete
vocabulary available", the world's most innovative puzzles, "100% graphics-free Infocom prose" and a
multiple SAVE feature, and naming its stable-mates ZORK, DEADLINE and STARCROSS. The last line before
the legal small print is the slogan **Step up to Infocom. All words. No pictures.**

## The box itself

**The front** is the title — **SUSPENDED**, with a red heartbeat trace running through it — over the
subtitle **A Cryogenic Nightmare**, above a photograph of a square white tray holding the mask of a
human face: pale, smooth, expressionless, yellow-green eyes open and looking straight out. The tray
sits on dark blue printed with a fine blue grid. **INFOCOM** is across the bottom.

**The back** is a photograph of the package laid out the way the box tells you to keep it — box,
computer keyboard, robot pieces, documentation — and its caption is both a joke and an instruction:

> Essential life-support mechanisms for cryogenically suspended persons: the computer keyboard,
> robot tracking devices, documentation. Disk and all can be stored in package when not in use.

Publisher line, from the foot of the back cover: **Infocom, Inc., 55 Wheeler Street, Cambridge, MA
02138**, **Copyright © 1983 Infocom, Inc.**, the trademark notice *"INFOCOM® is a registered trademark
of Infocom, Inc. DEADLINE, STARCROSS, SUSPENDED, and INTERLOGIC are trademarks of Infocom, Inc."*,
**Manufactured and printed in U.S.A.**, plus the standard **Limited Warranty** and **Copyright** blocks.
No platform, disk format, part number or ISBN is legible in these scans; the copy speaks only of
"the disk".

## What was on the table

Three kinds of paper and a sheet of pieces. The briefing booklet was the job description — crew roster,
the CLC peripherals, the three surface systems. The letters supplied the story of Gregory Franklin and
the one procedure that had to be carried out in a particular way; the lottery card was the character
sheet. The schematic was the site plan, and the six die-cut pieces were counters, one per robot. The
manual proper, the one with a table of contents on page 11, is the notable absence from these scans.
